\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}

Several limitations bound the strength of the present claims, and each points to a concrete next step.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{What clearing the external benchmark's naive baseline is worth here (added 14.08.2026).} The
model reaches a normalized rank correlation of $0.278$ on that benchmark against zero for its
mean-effects baseline, and that margin is weaker evidence than it appears. The benchmark is run
leave-cell-line-out, and a held-out line is unseen, so the baseline has no line effect to use: its
fitted values vary only by compound and by fold, never by cell line --- five distinct values within
each compound, one per fold. It is competing with one of its two channels removed, and clearing a
baseline in that condition is close to automatic for any model carrying signal at all.

The benchmark's own leave-pairs-out mode restores that channel, and there a predictor knowing only
each line's average response reaches $0.322$ on the within-compound scale. \textbf{Whether this model
clears a fully-armed mean-effects baseline is therefore not established}, and it is the single most
informative measurement that remains: it requires retraining under a leave-pairs-out split, which is
not attempted here. This is stated rather than left implicit because the published margin invites the
stronger reading.

It also bears on the choice of metric. Reporting a within-compound correlation rather than the
benchmark's normalized one could look like avoiding the very correction that benchmark exists to
apply. Measured on the same predictions, it is not: under this split design the normalization removes
the compound effect and nothing else, so the two nearly coincide, moving the within-compound figure
from $0.242$ to $0.269$. The pooled correlation of $0.771$ is the flattering number and is not the one
this work reports.
\end{revisionblock}

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{A set of training hyperparameters was fixed by convention and never varied (added 14.08.2026).}
The learning rate, the gradient-clipping threshold, the learning-rate scheduler's patience and factor,
and the early-stopping patience are conventional defaults carried from the first prototype. None is
taken from the literature, none was tuned, and none has been varied on these data. This is a weaker
footing than the settings around them, which is why it is stated rather than left implicit: the number
of epochs rests on a measurement of where training peaks, the absence of weight decay on a measurement
of what decay does under this optimizer, the decayed-parameter grouping on a named software convention,
and the standardization clip on the default of an established package.

It is also not covered by the model-side ablation reported above. That varied regularization strength,
network capacity, batch size and sample reweighting, and found every axis flat; the learning rate, the
clip and the scheduler were not among its axes, so its conclusion does not extend to them. Two of the
five could move a reported quantity rather than only the running time. The learning rate sets where
training peaks, and the epoch at which each representation peaks is used above as evidence about how
linearly accessible that representation is; the early-stopping patience decides when a run ends, and
every run recorded here ended by early stopping rather than by exhausting its epoch budget. The
remaining three are inert by comparison.

They are left at their values rather than chosen now. Selecting them after the results are visible
would be tuning against the answer, and the margins this report treats as informative are small enough
that such tuning could manufacture one. The honest statement is that the comparison is reported at one
untuned setting, applied identically to both arms --- which is what makes it a fair comparison rather
than a strong one.
\end{revisionblock}

\paragraph{The response label is a fitted quantity, and its uncertainty is discarded.}
The target is not a measurement but a parameter of a curve fitted to one. DrEval re-fit every
dose-response curve with CurveCurator, normalizing each replicate against its own no-drug control and
folding replicate variability \emph{into} the fit rather than averaging replicates beforehand, on the
argument that averaging ``leads to inaccurate or misleading drug response measures in the case of
large discrepancies between replicates'' (Section~\ref{sec:target}). That is the better of the two
treatments of replicate disagreement, and it is why this source was adopted. It has two consequences the
present model does not handle.

First, \textbf{the label-noise ceiling is no longer measurable in the obvious way.} Under the previous
source two experiments produced two numbers that could be compared; under this one there is a single fit
per (cell line, compound) and no pair remains to disagree. An earlier estimate of that disagreement
therefore does not transfer. \revision{\textbf{Corrected 14.08.2026.} This paragraph continued
``CurveCurator reports a per-curve uncertainty that would supply the estimate; it is not currently
read''. It is read now: the per-curve fit statistics are analysed in Section~\ref{sec:results}, where
$10.9\,\%$ of the fits behind this panel prove not to be statistically significant and the per-compound
rate of such fits predicts out-of-fold performance. What remains unquantified is narrower --- the share
of the residual error that is irreducible --- since fit quality bounds the labels without giving the
noise floor directly.}

Second, \textbf{every label is treated as equally certain.} The masked loss weights each observed (cell,
compound) pair by the density term alone; a curve fitted from concordant replicates and one fitted from
badly discordant replicates enter the objective with identical weight. Propagating the fit uncertainty
into the loss --- as an inverse-variance weight alongside the existing term --- is a small change to the
weighting already implemented and would let the model discount the labels that deserve it. It has not
been done, and no result here should be read as though label quality were uniform.

A constraint on that regeneration is worth stating in advance, because it determines what the comparison
can conclude. Two changes must never be made in the same run: on the evidence of this project, a change
of target and a change of architecture applied together leave the outcome unattributable, and the
collapse that was initially read as a statement about the representations turned out to be a property of
the objective. Whatever scGPT-versus-PCA margin replaces the withdrawn one must be established over
multiple seeds before it is reported as a margin at all, since the margins measured on this data have
been of the same order as the seed-to-seed variation.
\revision{\emph{A characterisation of the previous margin was removed here (12.08.2026).} It stated that
the margin was not sign-consistent across seeds and did not survive the normalized metric. The project
record states the opposite on the first point --- the three-seed check it refers to was sign-consistent

reading was right, so it is kept and the characterisation is not.}
The external benchmark is also incomplete, covering only the leave-cell-line-out split rather than the
leave-tissue-out and leave-drug-out settings.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Reproducibility, and the standing of the numbers below (05.08.2026).} A review of the
preprocessing pipeline found several sources of run-to-run variation that had not been controlled. They
are recorded here rather than beside any one result because they qualify all of them. Random seeds were
set in the training code but not everywhere randomness was actually consumed: the principal-component
decomposition silently used the library default rather than the project seed, the training data loaders
took their shuffling order from the global generator instead of an explicit one, and the embedding script
set no seed at all. All are now fixed to a single project-wide value.

This matters most for scGPT, whose embedding path is not deterministic by construction. The model reads
at most 1{,}200 genes per cell and samples them at random when a cell has more, so the all-genes variant
is a random subsample of each cell's transcriptome rather than the transcriptome itself. With the seed
fixed it is one reproducible draw, and it is treated here as a sanity check rather than as a
full-transcriptome comparator --- a single well-defined input is preferable to averaging over draws,
which would multiply the cost of every experiment and leave each individual result harder to attribute.

The hardware also bounds what can be checked. FlashAttention is CUDA-only and cannot be installed on this
machine, so scGPT runs through the standard attention implementation; this is numerically equivalent but
is why embedding one gene-set variant takes about twenty minutes here, and why the variants are not
regenerated on demand. Training on the MPS backend is not bit-reproducible: repeated identical runs of
the PCA arm returned different Spearman correlations while every other arm was exact, so differences
below the run-to-run band are not interpretable.
\revision{\emph{The width of that band is withdrawn (12.08.2026).} Four correlations were quoted here to
size it. They were measured on the retired target and the voided compound panel, from representations
that predate the preprocessing corrections, so they no longer describe this pipeline; the values are
kept as a record in \code{docs/steps/corrections-and-dead-ends.md}. That MPS is non-deterministic is a
property of the backend and stands, but \emph{how large} the resulting band is has to be re-measured
before any difference is called interpretable or uninterpretable against it.}

A second asymmetry concerns how each representation is built, and it bears on the comparison itself. The
principal components were fitted on the full expression matrix: gene selection, per-gene standardization
and the rotation all used every cell, held-out lines included. The scGPT embedding has no step of that
kind: it receives the same expression matrix, but its weights are pretrained and frozen, and the binning
that precedes them uses only the cell being embedded, so nothing about it is \emph{estimated across
cells}. Section~\ref{sec:data} sets out operation by operation what the two arms share and where the
single difference lies. So the two arms were not on equal terms. None of these fits sees a response
label, which is why standard pipelines accept them, but the PCA baseline was tuned to the cells it was
evaluated on and the scGPT representation was not. The bias runs toward the control, so any scGPT
advantage measured this way is conservative on that account.

\suggestion{A further limitation concerns the loss rather than the labels. The response values of a given
compound are not uniformly distributed over the cell lines: most lines cluster in a narrow band and the
pharmacologically interesting extremes are sparse. Under an unweighted squared-error objective the dense
region dominates the gradient, and the optimal predictor shrinks toward each compound's mean, which is
exactly the behaviour observed here (predicted standard deviations of 0.53 and 0.47 against a true spread
of 1.0). This is the regression analogue of class imbalance, and the established remedy is to weight each
observation by the inverse of a smoothed estimate of the label density, so that rare response values
contribute in proportion to their scarcity rather than their frequency \cite{dir,denseweight}. This was
implemented and tested, and it does not help; the result is reported above. The shrinkage is therefore
not an artifact of how the objective weights the response range, which leaves the small number of
independent cell lines as the remaining explanation and makes the case for acting on the label side
rather than on the loss.}
\revision{\emph{Correction, 12.08.2026.} Two things in the passage above do not stand. The predicted
and true spreads quoted were measured on a per-drug standardised target that has since been retired,
which is why the true spread reads as exactly one; they should have been removed when the other
quantitative claims were, and are withdrawn here. More seriously, the inference does not follow from
the evidence. Whether the weighting helped was judged on rank correlation and on mean squared error,
and both are structurally insensitive to precisely what the weighting alters: a change that widens the
predictions without reordering them leaves rank correlation untouched, and squared error is minimised
by the shrunken predictor in the first place. The project's own records show the mechanism did operate,
the predicted spread widening under weighting while the ordering stayed put. So the correct statement
is that the intervention did not improve the quantities that were measured, not that shrinkage is
independent of how the objective weights the response range. That question is reopened, and is
answered only once a calibration measure sits alongside the rank and error metrics.}

the training target can be aligned with the evaluation by regressing on the doubly normalized residual, in
which both the drug and the cell-line mean effects are removed, with the means estimated only on the
training lines of each fold; this matches the quantity DrEval rewards.

Second, whether the single-cell resolution earns itself is an
open question: a model that predicts the line label from a bag of its cells is the natural test.

\suggestion{This point deserves to be stated more sharply, because it bears directly on the second
research question. The pharmacological label is constant within a cell line, and the masked objective
therefore requires every cell of a line to regress onto the same scalar. Any difference the model
predicts between two cells of the same line is scored as error, so the loss actively penalizes the
within-line variation that the single-cell framing exists to capture; under this objective the optimal
per-cell predictor is constant across a line, and heterogeneity can only appear as failure to fit. It
follows that the present setup does not test whether heterogeneity is learned implicitly \textemdash{} it
is structurally unable to. Multiple-instance learning is not merely an incremental architecture to try
but the minimal change that makes the question askable: predicting the line label from a bag of cells
constrains only the aggregate, leaves the model free to assign different values to different cells, and
so exposes a per-cell prediction as a readout of which subpopulation drives the response. That
readout is the quantity of clinical interest, since relapse is driven by rare surviving subpopulations
rather than by the population mean, and it is the natural point at which to connect the model to the
recurrent heterogeneity programs annotated for this dataset \cite{scp542}. Note also that the per-cell
loss currently weights each cell line by how many cells were sequenced from it, which ranges from 56 to
1{,}990 and is an artifact of the assay rather than information about the label; weighting each line
equally is a prerequisite for any of these comparisons to be read as biology.} Third, and most directly, the ceiling can be raised by adding
independent cell lines, as CTRPv2 alone screens roughly 1{,}100 against the \NLines{} in the current
overlap. Beyond these, a diagnostic error analysis of where the model fails is low-risk and immediately
useful, whereas gene-level interpretation to surface transcriptomic drivers of resistance is worthwhile
only once per-drug performance is substantially higher and stable, so that the interpretation does not
merely describe noise. The longer horizon remains a cross-database, multi-task model spanning
efficacy and toxicity, fine-tunable on clinical outcomes.

\begin{suggestionblock}
The order of the next two steps is deliberate rather than a matter of convenience. \textbf{Multiple-instance
learning} comes first because it is the minimal change that makes the second research question answerable
at all: under the present objective every cell of a line is regressed onto the same scalar, so within-line
variation is scored as error rather than as the quantity of interest. Predicting a line's response from a
bag of its cells constrains only the aggregate, leaving the model free to differ between cells of one
line. It requires no additional data.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Matching the input regularizer in effect rather than in nominal value (added 14.08.2026).}
Both arms are trained with the same input-dropout rate, and a six-point sweep across the range from
zero to three tenths, at three seeds and both representations, shows that the rate does not act on
them alike: the transcript-level arm is flat across the whole range, within a single seed's spread,
while the principal-component arm varies by an order of magnitude more. Its dimensions are ordered by
variance, so dropping coordinates at a fixed rate removes a heavier share of the signal from it than
from an embedding whose coordinates carry comparable weight; whether that is regularization or
deletion of signal is not established. The setting is left as shipped, and every margin in this report
is quoted at it and is conservative with respect to it --- the principal-component arm scores its best
at a lower rate, so equalizing would widen rather than narrow the reported gap. The experiment worth
running is therefore not another sweep, which exists, but a per-arm rate chosen so that the
\emph{expected variance removed} matches rather than the nominal rate. It is listed here rather than
among the limitations because nothing reported depends on it being done.
\end{revisionblock}

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Fine-tuning the transcript-level encoder is the step this report's own finding points at, and
it was missing from this outlook (added 14.08.2026).} The embedding used here is frozen and
general-purpose: it maps cells onto a shared biological manifold and suppresses the line and batch
identity that distinguishes them. Section~\ref{sec:results} finds that this is precisely why it
predicts less well than the fitted baseline, because the response label is defined per cell line, so
the structure the encoder removes is the structure the label varies over. The direct test of that
account is therefore to stop freezing it --- to fine-tune the encoder on this task and ask whether the
ordering reverses. Nothing here does that, and the outlook listed cross-database integration, bulk
pretraining and clinical fine-tuning while omitting the one experiment that follows immediately from
the result. It is stated as the obvious next step and not as a prediction: an encoder fine-tuned on
roughly a hundred and fifty independent labels may simply memorize them, which is the same
small-sample objection that governs everything else here.
\end{revisionblock}

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Re-running the comparison under the benchmark's leave-pairs-out design (added 14.08.2026).}
This is listed first among the next steps because it is the cheapest and it bears directly on how the
present result should be read. The external benchmark defines four test designs and this work has
used one. Under the design used here a naive mean-effects baseline cannot express a cell-line effect,
so clearing it establishes less than it appears to; under leave-pairs-out it can, and a predictor
knowing only each line's average response reaches a within-compound rank correlation of $0.322$ ---
of the same order as everything reported here. Retraining both representations under that design
would answer whether the model clears a baseline with both of its channels available, which is the
question the benchmark was built to ask and the one this work leaves open. It requires no new data
and no new model, only a different partition of the pairs, and its results would not be comparable
with those above, which is why it is a separate experiment rather than a revision of this one.
\end{revisionblock}
