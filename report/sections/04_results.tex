\subsection{Does a transcript-level embedding beat the standard reduction?}
\label{sec:results-q1}

Both arms are \NPca-dimensional, so the comparison is at matched width. Scoring is per drug over
held-out cell lines and then averaged over the eleven panel compounds, aggregated to the cell line and
pooled across folds; the convention is fixed in Section~\ref{sec:data} and the alternatives are
reported below.

\paragraph{The principal-component arm leads, and the margin depends on what is being asked.}
With a per-cell objective, \code{X\_pca} leads \code{X\_scGPT} by $+0.0317$ with a linear head and
$+0.0383$ with a $(128,64)$ trunk, each outside the wider of the two seed bands
(\code{panel\_arch\_summary.csv}, \code{4a}~\S C). Capacity therefore moves both arms together and
leaves the ordering intact. Head count does not disturb it either: a \NDrugsModelled{}-head model scored on the same
eleven compounds gives $+0.0479$ (linear) and $+0.0252$ (trunk).

\paragraph{The scoring set moves the answer further than either architectural axis.}
The same \NDrugsModelled{}-head model scored on all \NDrugsModelled{} compounds gives $+0.0231$ with a linear head and
$-0.0077$ with a trunk --- inside the seed band, with the sign reversed
(\code{panel\_heads\_summary.csv}, \code{4a}~\S D). The panel and the full catalogue disagree about
which compounds are measured, not about the model.

\paragraph{The objective is the largest single axis.}
At $\alpha=0.5$ with squared error, the margin is $+0.0827$ under the per-cell objective and
$+0.0265$ under the bag objective, so $68.0\,\%$ of it does not survive the change
(\code{panel\_metrics.csv}, \code{5\_evaluation}~\S1.8). The transcript-level arm \emph{gains} under
multiple-instance learning while the principal-component arm loses.

\paragraph{The lead is not an artefact of how few labels there are.}
Thinning the label supply inside each fold while holding the input, the architecture and the loss
fixed, the margin runs $+0.0036$ at 25 labelled lines and $+0.0090$ at 50 --- inside the seed band in
both cases, so the arms are indistinguishable there --- then $+0.0505$ at 75 and $+0.0317$ at the full
budget (\code{panel\_curve\_summary.csv}, \code{4a}~\S E). A small-data account predicts a margin that
\emph{shrinks} as labels are added; it grows.

\paragraph{Nor is it an artefact of how much data the projection was fitted on.}
The principal-component arm is fitted on these cells while the transcript-level embedding is frozen,
which raises the possibility that its advantage is adaptation to this atlas rather than a property of
the representation. Dropping cell lines entirely --- so that their cells leave the folds, the batches
and the projection's fitting set alike --- leaves the margin unchanged: $+0.0353$ with 62 lines
retained, $+0.0279$ with 94, and $+0.0336$ with all \NLinesCV{}. Halving the atlas does not erode the lead.
At 31 lines both arms score below zero, which reports the collapse of the study rather than a small
margin, and is not evidence either way. The full-size row reproduces the standard configuration
($+0.0336$ against $+0.0317$), so the comparison is measuring what it should. Removing cells is far
more damaging than removing their labels: at roughly twenty fitting lines this design scores $-0.05$
where thinning labels alone scored $+0.14$.

\paragraph{One regularizer penalizes one arm, and it makes the reported margin conservative.}
Input dropout zeroes each input coordinate independently at a rate of $0.1$. The transcript-level
embedding's dimensions are entangled and comparable in magnitude; the principal components are
variance-ordered, so the same nominal rate removes a heavier-tailed share. Sweeping the rate over
$\{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ leaves the transcript-level arm flat --- a range of $0.0020$
against a per-seed standard deviation of $0.0021$ --- while the principal-component arm varies over
$0.0145$, so the regularizer acts on one arm and not the other. The shipped rate of $0.1$ is the least
favourable of the six to the leading arm, and no rate above zero improves on $p=0$ by more than a
tenth of a seed standard deviation, so the setting is not doing the work a regularizer is meant to do. Every margin in this
section is therefore quoted at input dropout $0.1$ and is conservative with respect to this setting.
Because weight decay is zero, dropout is the model's only regularizer, so this is not a minor
hyperparameter; what the rate should be is left open.

\paragraph{Everything sits near the per-drug null.}
Across all \NDrugsModelled{} compounds the improvement over a per-drug constant runs $+0.00017$ to $+0.00043$, and
for the linear principal-component arm it is $-0.00005$, worse than the constant
(\code{panel\_heads\_summary.csv}). A ridge regression on cell-line-mean embeddings scores $0.2767$
against $0.2608$ for the best per-cell principal-component arm, and on an external protocol a per-drug
random forest matches the multi-task model to $0.0003$
(\code{panel\_arch\_summary.csv}; \code{dreval\_lco\_results.csv}). \textbf{On both an internal and an
external comparison, the multi-task architecture has not been shown to buy anything measurable over a
simple per-drug model.}

\paragraph{Why the transcript-level arm scores lower.}
The response label is defined per cell line, so the quantity a model must read is variation
\emph{between} lines. The two representations differ there before any parameter is fitted: measured on
the inputs alone, the between-line share of variance is $0.584$ for \code{X\_pca} against $0.539$ for
\code{X\_scGPT}, a between-to-within ratio of $1.405$ against $1.168$
(\code{stage0\_input\_ceiling.csv}). The principal-component arm carries about a fifth more of the
structure the label varies over. The same difference is visible directly in the cancer-type embeddings
of Section~\ref{sec:data}: the principal-component projection resolves the cell lines as separate
clusters, while the transcript-level embedding places them on one continuous manifold.

This is the embedding behaving as intended rather than failing. A pretrained single-cell embedding is
built to map cells onto a shared biological manifold and to suppress batch and cell-line identity;
that is the property it is valued for. The label used here sits at exactly the granularity that
suppression removes. The finding is therefore a mismatch between the embedding's training objective
and this task's label, and it should not be read as a statement about embedding quality in general.

\paragraph{It is not undertraining.}
The epoch at which validation loss is minimised does not track performance. With a linear head the
principal-component arm peaks at epoch~12 and scores highest ($0.2608$) while the transcript-level arm
peaks at epoch~2 ($0.2291$); with a trunk the ordering of epochs reverses ($1$ and $8$) and the
transcript-level arm scores lowest ($0.2047$). Within \emph{each} representation the linear head wins,
despite the two having opposite epoch behaviour, and training the transcript-level arm four times
longer under a trunk scores worse rather than better. A candidate explanation --- that the
$104\times$ difference in input magnitude between the arms drives this under one shared learning
rate --- was tested by rescaling the transcript-level embedding to the principal-component arm's
magnitude and re-running: the mean best epoch was unchanged ($6.73$ against $7.13$). The optimizer is
AdamW, which normalises each step by a running second moment of the gradient and is therefore
approximately invariant to a uniform rescaling of its input.

\paragraph{Why no arm scores well in absolute terms.}
Four measured reasons, none of which is the choice of representation. First, the effective sample is
\NLinesCV{} cell lines rather than \NCells{} cells: one label per (cell line, compound) is broadcast to
every cell of that line, so each fold fits on roughly a hundred independent labelled examples. Second, label quality
varies and measurably constrains what can be predicted. The response is a curve fitted by CurveCurator,
which reports the quality of each fit: across the 1{,}971 curves behind this panel the median $R^2$ is
$0.867$, but $22.2\,\%$ fall below $0.5$ and $10.9\,\%$ of the fits are not statistically significant,
so roughly one label in nine comes from a curve that does not establish a dose-response. Per compound,
the fraction of non-significant fits predicts out-of-fold performance ($\rho = -0.718$, $p = 0.013$ for
the principal-component arm over eleven compounds), and the spread of the label across cell lines
predicts it independently ($\rho \approx +0.60$, $p \approx 0.05$; the two are uncorrelated,
$p = 0.50$). One panel compound, cisplatin, has $73\,\%$ non-significant fits and a median $R^2$ of
$0.167$, and is among the worst predicted.\footnote{An earlier analysis of
\NRepPairs{} doubly screened (cell line, compound) pairs reported a median disagreement of
\RepDiffMed{} standard deviations. It does not transfer, for two reasons. It covers six cell lines
across the full compound catalogue rather than the panel --- only 62 of those pairs involve a panel
compound, where the disagreement is smaller --- and it was computed on the response source retired on
11.08.2026, under which two experiments produced two numbers that the pipeline averaged. The present
source re-fits every dose-response curve with CurveCurator, including replicate variability in the fit
rather than averaging replicates beforehand, so there is one fit per (cell line, compound) and no pair
remains to disagree. Measuring the ceiling on the current target would require CurveCurator's per-curve
fit uncertainty, or re-deriving replicate disagreement from the raw dose-response data.} Third, over the
full catalogue the improvement on a per-drug constant lies between $+0.00017$ and $+0.00043$, and is
negative for one arm. Fourth, a ridge regression on cell-line-mean embeddings and a per-drug random
forest each match or exceed the multi-task network. Taken together the first two identify the ceiling
as label supply and label quality, and the fourth confirms that model capacity is not the binding
constraint.

\subsection{Does a model trained on single cells learn heterogeneity implicitly?}
\label{sec:results-q2}

No per-cell labels exist: one response value per (cell line, compound) is broadcast onto every cell of
that line, so any within-line structure in the predictions is imposed by the model rather than shown to
it. The instrument is a staged set of nulls and comparisons
(\code{4b}, \code{q2\_verdict.csv}), run over three seeds on both representations.

Predicted within-line spread exceeds the per-cell model's on $100\,\%$ (\code{X\_pca}) and
$82.3\,\%$ (\code{X\_scGPT}) of (compound, line) pairs on all three seeds, and that structure
reproduces across seeds with median $\rho$ of $0.256$ and $0.861$. On the criterion as specified this
is positive for both arms.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{How much of the ranking is general fragility rather than compound-specific response (added
14.08.2026).} The external benchmark is run leave-cell-line-out, and under that design the question
cannot be asked: a held-out line has never been seen, so the benchmark's own cell-line-mean baseline
predicts a constant and scores zero, and its normalization has no line effect to remove. Run instead
in the benchmark's leave-pairs-out mode, where held-out pairs belong to lines present in training,
that baseline becomes informative. Knowing only each line's average response, and nothing whatever
about any compound, it reaches a mean within-compound rank correlation of $0.322$; knowing only the
lineage reaches $0.184$. The same quantity computed without the benchmark, by correlating each
compound's measured response against the line's mean over the remaining ten, gives $0.361$.

\textbf{The ranking of cell lines within a compound is therefore substantially a property of the
line rather than of the compound:} lines that are broadly sensitive rank low almost everywhere.
\textbf{These figures are not comparable with those reported above}, which come from the
leave-cell-line-out design in which the model predicts lines it has never encountered and the same
baseline scores zero. The comparison is not a contest between the two, and it is not offered as one.
What it establishes is the size of the channel: general fragility supplies rank information of the
same order as the total this work reports, so a model that cannot observe a line's mean and
nevertheless ranks lines may be recovering that fragility from expression rather than any
compound-specific biology. Separating the two is not attempted here and would require a decomposition
this work has deliberately not built, for reasons given in the methods.
\end{revisionblock}

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Which representation is the more bias-driven, and whether the comparison survives controlling
for it (added 14.08.2026).} If the principal-component arm leads because it exploits general
fragility more effectively, that is a different claim from its carrying more of what the label varies
over. Taking as a proxy for a line's general sensitivity its mean response over the \emph{other ten}
compounds, so that the compound being scored never enters its own proxy, the transcript-level arm's
predictions correlate with that proxy at $0.117$ and the principal-component arm's at $0.160$,
averaged over compounds, seeds and all six objective settings. \textbf{The transcript-level arm is the
less bias-driven of the two, in every one of the six}, which is what an embedding built to suppress
line identity should do and is the same mechanism by which it scores lower here.

Controlling for the proxy by rank-partial correlation leaves the ordering unchanged: $0.214$ against
$0.173$, with the principal-component arm ahead in all six settings by between $0.007$ and $0.068$.
\textbf{The lead is therefore not an artifact of differential fragility exploitation}, which closes
one further alternative explanation by elimination. The two columns should nevertheless be read
together: the control costs the principal-component arm $20.4\,\%$ of its raw score and the
transcript-level arm $19.9\,\%$, so approximately a fifth of each is fragility-aligned and the
\emph{proportion} is nearly identical. The finding is not that one arm is biased and the other clean.
The limits are the eleven compounds, a proxy built from those same eleven, and a control that is
linear in the ranks; the narrowest margin is close to a tie. Read from
\code{bias\_accounting.csv}, produced by \code{scripts/evaluation/bias\_accounting.py}, whose raw
within-compound column reproduces \code{panel\_leaderboard.csv} in all twelve arms.
\end{revisionblock}

\paragraph{The positive is weak, and the positive control is why.}
Stage 7 asks whether the instrument can detect a between-line difference it already knows is present.
The median within-bag AUROC is $0.518$ and $0.537$ against a $0.5$ null, with fewer than half of pairs
reaching significance, and the distribution is close to uniform
(Figure~\ref{fig:q2instrument}). For the principal-component arm, technical covariates explain
$82\,\%$ as much variance as the signal reproduces across seeds. The structure is not noise; it is not
established to be biological.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{The confound veto was evaluated and does not fire, for either arm (added 14.08.2026).} The
criterion's final stage regresses each cell's predicted response on sequencing depth, genes detected,
mitochondrial fraction and cell-cycle score within its line, and vetoes the result if those covariates
explain as much of the within-line variation as reproduces across seeds. The bar is a comparison
rather than a constant, and was fixed before any model was trained: the adjusted coefficient of
determination from that regression is set against the squared cross-seed correlation, so both
quantities are variance fractions of the same predictions. A permutation null cannot supply such a
magnitude, because with several hundred cells per line a coefficient far too small to matter is still
significant against one. Measured, the covariates explain $0.053$ against a reproduced $0.065$ for the
principal-component arm and $0.266$ against $0.741$ for the transcript-level arm --- $82\,\%$ and
$36\,\%$ of the bar. Neither is vetoed, which is the condition under which the positive above may be
stated at all.

Two things follow that are worth stating rather than leaving to be inferred. The principal-component
arm clears the bar at $82\,\%$, so its within-line signal should be described as not
confound-\emph{dominated} rather than as confound-\emph{free}. And the two arms order oppositely on
the two quantities: the transcript-level arm has five times the absolute confound coefficient and
still clears the bar more comfortably, because what it reproduces across seeds is so much larger. A
bar stated as an absolute coefficient rather than as a ratio would therefore have reversed which arm
survives --- which is why the form was fixed in advance rather than after the numbers were visible.
\end{revisionblock} Whether the variation is genuine \emph{drug-response} heterogeneity, and
whether it predicts which cells survive, are not addressed and cannot be with this data.

\subsection{What the comparison cannot settle}
\label{sec:results-limits}

\paragraph{The loss comparison is unresolved.}
Under the pre-registered decision rule, no arm of the six-arm sweep displaces the
unweighted absolute-error arm: all thirteen challengers fail a guard. The arm with the highest rank
correlation in the sweep ($0.2824$) is among them, failing on calibration and absolute error --- rank
bought at the cost of both is not declared better. The comparison is additionally not robust to two
things that ought not to matter: the scoring convention changes which arm ranks first, and one
configuration ($\alpha=0$ with squared error on \code{X\_pca}) does not reproduce across identical
runs, spanning $0.2473$--$0.2541$ over two executions while thirteen of fourteen arms are identical to
six decimals (\code{panel\_execution\_band.csv}).

\paragraph{The two arms are not optimised comparably.}
\code{X\_pca} reaches the optimizer at $104\times$ the magnitude of \code{X\_scGPT} under one shared
learning rate (\code{input\_scale.csv}). Training peaks accordingly and inconsistently: with a trunk
the principal-component arm peaks at epoch~1 and the transcript-level arm at~8, and with a linear head
the ordering reverses. Label supply is excluded as the cause, since it moves the peak in opposite
directions for the two arms. This is an optimisation artefact rather than a property of either
representation, and it is the strongest remaining threat to the comparison in this section.
