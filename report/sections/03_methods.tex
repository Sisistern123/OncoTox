\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

All experiments use the highest-confidence intersection of SCP542 \cite{scp542} and CTRPv2 \cite{ctrpv2,ctrp_rees}. This intersection comprises \NLines{} cell lines with
post-quality-control measurements (190 roster name matches, of which 10 are unscreened), \NDrugs{}
compounds and \NCells{} cells, with effectively complete viability coverage inside the overlap. Genes are
reduced to the \NHvg{} most highly variable; scGPT embeds the \NVocab{} of these that lie in its
vocabulary, while PCA is computed on all \NHvg{}.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Quality control inherited from the source (added 05.08.2026).} SCP542 does not arrive raw, and
this project applies no cell-level filtering of its own, so the figures above are the output of the
source study's quality control rather than a raw measurement \cite{scp542}. Cells were profiled in nine
multiplexed pools on the 10x Chromium platform, at an average of about 280 cells per line; barcode
filtering, alignment and molecule counting used Cell Ranger 3.0.1. Cells were assigned to cell lines by
the consensus of a genetic and an expression-based method, with inconsistent assignments --- largely
cells of low sequencing coverage --- discarded. The number of genes with a non-zero measurement in a
given cell, its detected-gene count, served as the quality proxy, and cells were retained when that
count fell between 2{,}000 and 9{,}000. The two bounds catch different failures: a count below the
floor indicates that almost no RNA was captured, as in an empty droplet carrying only ambient
transcript or a cell whose contents have leaked out, while a count above the ceiling indicates that the
droplet held two cells rather than one, since two transcriptomes together present more distinct genes
than either alone. The window therefore retains droplets consistent with a single intact cell.
Suspected doublets and low-quality cells were removed on this basis, as were cell lines with fewer than
fifty assigned cells. What
remains is the \NCells{} cells and 198 cell lines used here, at an average of 19{,}264 unique molecular
identifiers and 3{,}802 detected genes per cell. The gene filters the source study applied for its own
heterogeneity analyses were \emph{not} applied to the distributed matrix, which retains \NGenes{}
genes, so the gene selection described below is independent of them.

No further quality control is performed here, and none is warranted. The doublet call in particular
rests on information the distributed matrix does not carry: because cells were assigned to lines by
genotype, a droplet containing two different cell lines presents mixed variants and is identified
directly, which is a stronger discriminator than expression-based inference and cannot be reconstructed
from expression alone. Applying a second, generic filtering pass would remove genuine cells at the
margins of an already filtered population without recovering that signal. Two consequences of the
inherited filter are worth stating. Cells outside the retained window are absent from the data
entirely, so any statement this report makes about cellular heterogeneity concerns heterogeneity within
that window --- relevant to the second research question, since states of low transcript content, such
as quiescent or senescence-like cells, lie near its lower bound. And the 198 lines are where the sample
funnel begins: \NLines{} of them carry usable response measurements, and that number, not the size of
the drug screen, is what bounds the effective sample size, since the screen itself covers roughly
1{,}100 lines.

The source study additionally centers expression before its own analyses, in two forms, and neither is
adopted here. Centering each gene on its mean across all cells is already subsumed by the per-gene
standardization described below, which centers and scales. Centering each gene within each cell line is
a different operation: it removes between-line variation and retains only variation within a line.
Because the response label is constant across the cells of a line, all of its signal is between-line,
so per-line centering would remove precisely the variance the target depends upon. It is nevertheless
the natural representation for the second research question, and the point at which to revisit it is
the move to pooling over a line's cells rather than predicting from each cell separately.

\textbf{Expression preprocessing (revised 05.08.2026).} SCP542 is distributed as counts per million, so
library-size normalization has already been applied --- once, to the full gene matrix, which is where it
belongs. The pipeline therefore does not normalize a second time. Expression is transformed as
$E_{ij} = \log_{2}(1 + \mathrm{CPM}_{ij}/10)$, which is the quantification the dataset's own authors
use: they divide by ten because the average number of unique molecular identifiers detected per cell is
below $10^5$, and without it the difference between detected and undetected genes is inflated
\cite{scp542}. The \NHvg{} most variable genes are selected on that matrix using the dispersion-based
Seurat criterion; each gene is then standardized to zero mean and unit variance across cells, with
$z$-scores clipped at 10, before the principal components are taken
(\code{scripts/preprocessing/expression.py}, \code{scripts/preprocessing/scp542\_conversion.py},
\code{scripts/preprocessing/add\_pca.py}). Until this revision both steps used a plain $\log(1+x)$ on
raw CPM --- natural log, no divisor --- which has no justification specific to this data, whereas the
published choice is argued from a measured property of it.

The reason for transforming at all, rather than passing counts per million directly to the
decomposition, is that principal component analysis and every method built on Euclidean distance weigh
each gene by its absolute spread. On the untransformed scale a housekeeping gene at 5{,}000 counts per
million varying by a fifth separates two cells by a thousand units, while a transcription factor at
fifty counts per million varying by a factor of two separates them by fifty; the leading components
then report which cells carried more ribosomal and mitochondrial transcript, not which cells differ
biologically. Expression differences are multiplicative, and the logarithm converts a fold change into
a fixed additive distance wherever on the scale it occurs, so a doubling counts equally in a rare and
in an abundant gene. The per-gene standardization described next equalizes variance as well, but does
not substitute for the transform: gene selection precedes it and its dispersion statistic is defined on
the logarithmic scale, and standardizing a strongly right-skewed distribution still leaves a few
extreme cells dominating the gene.

Until this revision the PCA branch did two further things differently. It applied a second library-size
normalization \emph{after} gene selection, dividing each cell by the total of its retained genes alone,
which rescales a cell by the share of its expression that happens to fall inside the selected set and
scales the same cell differently in each gene-set variant. And it applied no per-gene standardization,
so the leading components tracked absolute expression level rather than variation between cells. Both
are corrected in the code; \textbf{every PCA number in this report was produced before the correction}
and will change when the representation is recomputed. The change of log transform may additionally
move which genes the variability criterion selects, since it alters the expression bins the dispersions
are normalized within, so the gene set itself is not guaranteed to be identical either.

\textbf{The two representations are preprocessed differently, and deliberately so.} scGPT receives the
same counts-per-million matrix with neither the logarithm nor the per-gene standardization applied. The
embedding entry point used here, \code{embed\_data}, exposes no normalization or transform argument at
all: the single operation applied to expression before the model reads it is a binning of each cell into
51 bins whose edges are quantiles of that same cell's non-zero values
(\code{scgpt/tasks/cell\_emb.py}, \code{scgpt/data\_collator.py}, \code{scgpt/preprocess.py} in the
vendored scGPT checkout; the call site is \code{scripts/preprocessing/gen\_embeds.py}). scGPT's separate
\code{Preprocessor} utility, which its fine-tuning tutorials use and which does normalize to a fixed
library size and select variable genes, is not on this path \cite{scgpt}.

Nothing is lost by omitting them, because binning is a rank transform. The bin edges are quantiles of the
cell's own values and each value is digitized against those same edges, so a strictly increasing map
applied within a cell --- dividing by the library size, taking a logarithm, or both --- moves the values
and the edges together and every gene lands in the bin it started in. Raw unique-molecular-identifier
counts, counts per million, and $\log(1+x)$ of either therefore present the model with identical input,
up to floating-point ties between values a transform renders equal, which the binning breaks at random
under the fixed seed. Normalizing before scGPT is not merely unnecessary; it is inert.

This is why the asymmetry is not an oversight. The principal-component baseline requires the logarithm
and the standardization because it measures Euclidean distance on the expression values themselves,
where each gene's influence scales with its absolute spread --- the argument given above. scGPT never
reads those values, only their order within each cell, so the same two operations would alter nothing it
sees. Applying them to both arms for the sake of symmetry would not make the comparison fairer; it would
only disguise the fact that the two representations consume different information from the same matrix.
It also means the choice of source matrix is settled for one arm and open for the other: the portal
additionally distributes the pre-quality-control unique-molecular-identifier counts, and substituting
them would leave the scGPT input bit-for-bit unchanged while changing gene selection and the principal
components.
\end{revisionblock}

A property of the data governs every error estimate in this report. The label is defined per cell line and
drug, not per cell, so all cells of a line share the same broadcast bulk value and act as
pseudo-replicates. The effective sample size is therefore the number of cell lines, approximately
\NIndep{} after the held-out splits are removed, not the \NCells{} cells. Treating cells as independent
would understate the uncertainty by roughly the square root of the number of cells per line.

\subsection{Drug panel selection}
\label{sec:panel}

The compounds a multi-task model predicts are a methodological choice, not a detail of setup, and the
choice made here is deliberately independent of the response values the model is trained on. Two earlier
panels in this project were selected using those values --- one by a differential-response gate requiring
a compound both to kill and to spare a population of cell lines, the other by ranking candidates on the
same quantity before a literature criterion was applied --- and both were discarded. The gate is
instructive because its defect is systematic rather than incidental: requiring absolute potency excludes
every cytostatic agent by construction, since a compound that arrests rather than kills never crosses the
viability threshold however sensitive the line, and a large share of targeted therapy is cytostatic. More
generally, selecting compounds on the spread of our own labels enriches for drugs that happen to separate
our particular cell lines, which flatters every correlation subsequently measured on them, and it requires
reading labels of cell lines that are later held out.

The panel is therefore defined by three conditions, of which only the second involves our data at all.
First, the compound must be approved by the FDA for a cancer indication, taken from the systematic
enumeration of Sun et al.\ \cite{sun_fda}, which lists 150 drugs approved between 1949 and 2014 together
with approval year, therapeutic class and target gene. Second, CTRPv2 must have screened it against at
least 90\% of the cell lines in the intersection with SCP542; this threshold is where the coverage
distribution breaks, and the break is sharp: 45 candidates lie at or above it, the lowest at 91.2\%,
while the next compound down reaches only 69.1\% and the tail falls to 28.7\%. Third,
a published claim about the compound's response determinants must exist and must be verified against the
source, recorded per drug together with what that source actually establishes. The first two conditions
are computed; the third is a literature judgement that cannot be, and is carried as a table whose entries
the code requires to lie inside the set the first two produce.

Applying these gives the following funnel. Of the 150 approved drugs, 120 have a small-molecule structure;
the remaining 30 are monoclonal antibodies, enzymes, a cell therapy and a radiopharmaceutical, which
cannot appear in a small-molecule viability screen. Of those 120, 57 were screened by CTRPv2, 45 clear the
coverage threshold, and 11 carry a verified published claim. The panel is \code{gemcitabine},
\code{doxorubicin}, \code{etoposide}, \code{paclitaxel} and \code{cisplatin} among cytotoxics, and
\code{imatinib}, \code{sorafenib}, \code{dasatinib}, \code{erlotinib}, \code{crizotinib} and
\code{afatinib} among targeted agents, with coverage between 91.2\% and 98.3\% of the available cell
lines.

Matching an external drug list to a screen is not a string operation, and treating it as one loses
compounds systematically. Approval lists name drugs by international nonproprietary name and often in
their salt form, whereas screens use development codes and free bases; furthermore the compound
annotation and the response values reach this project through two different files that name the same 545
compounds differently, so that a name-based join between them fails for 102 of the 545, of which 15 are
single-agent approved or clinical compounds. Four keys are therefore used and the successful one is
recorded per compound: the two name spaces, the PubChem compound identifier, and PubChem's
parent-compound relation \cite{pubchem}, which is what connects a salt named in an approval list to the
free base a screen dissolved. The last key alone accounts for 13 of the matches, among them imatinib and
doxorubicin. The join between the two internal files is made on the screen's own compound identifier
rather than on names, which matches all 545. One compound required an explicit correction: cisplatin is
recorded under a nonstandard name and carries a structure identifier pointing to elemental platinum
rather than to the drug, and is resolved here on the screen's own recorded structure and vendor
catalogue entry.

Platinum agents are represented once rather than three times, although cisplatin, carboplatin and
oxaliplatin are all screened with near-identical coverage. Cisplatin and carboplatin share the
\emph{cis}-diammine carrier ligand and form the same 1,2-intrastrand adduct; they are largely
cross-resistant and are governed by the same repair biology, so including both would spend two positions
in an eleven-drug panel on one resistance mechanism. Oxaliplatin's diaminocyclohexane adduct evades
mismatch repair and is genuinely complementary, but its expression-level determinant is the least
established of the three. Cisplatin was selected as the representative of the class.

One entry is reported as contested rather than as supporting evidence, because the panel should not
appear better grounded than it is. The compound in question is cisplatin, which is itself named in two
of the four papers underlying this work --- it is one of the five drugs the single-cell benchmark of
Chen et al.\ \cite{scdeal} is built on, and a perturbation tested against expression heterogeneity by
Kinker et al.\ \cite{scp542} --- so what is contested is its response determinant rather than its
standing in the relevant literature. The standard determinant of platinum resistance is ERCC1, and the two
directly comparable cell-line studies disagree: ERCC1 mRNA correlates with cisplatin resistance in
cervical carcinoma lines \cite{britten_ercc1}, while no association is found across twenty lung cancer
lines for cisplatin or carboplatin \cite{shimizu_ercc1}. Neither supersedes the other, as they differ in
lineage and assay, and the more recent is also the less cited. The likely explanation is that ERCC1 has
four splice isoforms of which only one performs DNA repair, and available assays cannot distinguish them
\cite{friboulet_ercc1}. This bears directly on the present work: the features used here are gene-level
expression, in which the functional isoform is not representable, so poor performance on cisplatin is
anticipated by the literature rather than diagnostic of the model. Retaining the compound with its
determinant marked contested is preferred to restricting the panel to drugs whose determinants are
settled, which would bias the evaluation toward compounds whose answer is already known. By the same
reasoning the evidence supporting doxorubicin and etoposide, both substrates of the ABCB1 efflux
transporter \cite{skinner_abcb1,omori_abcb1}, is recorded with its setting, and topoisomerase expression
is not claimed as a determinant for either, since it does not correlate with sensitivity where it has
been measured \cite{omori_abcb1}.

Finally, the availability of a fitted IC50 is deliberately excluded from the selection criteria. An IC50
is undefined when a dose-response curve does not cross half-maximal viability, so the proportion of cell
lines for which one exists is a measure of a compound's potency, ranging within this panel from 14 to 174
of the available lines. Selecting on it would reintroduce the potency filter described above under the
appearance of a data-quality rule. Where the two response measures are compared, the comparison is
restricted at evaluation to compounds with sufficient IC50 coverage, with the per-compound counts
reported. The selection is implemented in
\code{notebooks/2\_drug\_selection.ipynb}, which reads only the response table and writes
the panel with its citations to \code{notebooks/outputs/panel/panel.csv}; the compound identifier
resolution is in \code{scripts/annotation/drug\_annotation.py} and
\code{scripts/sources/pubchem.py}.

\subsection{Representation and model}

The comparison between representations is controlled so that only the content of the embedding differs.
Both the PCA baseline (\code{X\_pca}) and the scGPT embedding (\code{X\_scGPT}) are 512-dimensional and are
fed into the same multi-head perceptron, with hidden dimensions 128 and 64, layer normalization, GELU
activations and dropout. The network has one output head per drug and is trained with a masked
mean-squared-error loss that is evaluated only on observed cell-drug pairs. The model and training code are
defined in \code{scripts/model/OncoMLP.py} and \code{scripts/training/train\_multitask.py}.

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Splits, and where each representation is fitted (added 05.08.2026).} Train, validation and test
are a 70/15/15 partition of cell lines, so every cell of a line falls on the same side and no line is
split across parts. The assignment is \emph{frozen to a versioned file}
(\code{splits/split\_ctrp.csv}) instead of being redrawn from the data on each run. It has to be:
eligibility depends on which compounds survive the label filters, so a change to the drug panel would
otherwise move lines between train, validation and test unannounced, and results from either side of
that change would look comparable while being scored on different held-out lines. Redrawing requires an
explicit flag, and a line that the frozen file does not cover is an error rather than a fresh draw.
\revision{\emph{Correction, 12.08.2026.} The mechanism is as described, but the file itself had never

The principal components used as model input are fitted on the training lines of that split alone ---
per-gene standardization and the rotation both --- and then applied to the held-out lines
(\code{X\_pca\_train\_ctrp}). A separate all-cells decomposition (\code{X\_pca}) is kept for description
only, such as the latent-space figures, where holding cells out would be wrong. The cross-validation
results are the exception: its folds are drawn at training time rather than stored, so no precomputed
train-only projection exists for them and they still use the all-cells decomposition. scGPT needs no
such distinction --- its weights are pretrained and frozen, and its per-cell binning uses only the cell
being embedded, so nothing about it is fitted on this data.
\end{revisionblock}

\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Early stopping is nested inside the training lines (added 12.08.2026).} Training runs for a
fixed maximum number of epochs and the checkpoint with the lowest validation error is restored, so the
stopping epoch is a quantity chosen from data. Until 12.08.2026 the cross-validation harness measured
that error on the fold it was about to score, which makes every reported figure a minimum over up to
twenty-five epochs on its own evaluation data. Each fold now withholds fifteen per cent of its training
cell lines, grouped as everywhere else, as the early-stopping set
(\code{scripts/training/cv.py::inner\_holdout}), so the scored fold decides nothing about the model that
predicts it; the per-drug-mean baseline and the head initialisation are fitted on the remaining fitting
lines for the same reason. This is the arrangement the DrEval benchmark itself uses, whose per-fold
splits are three-way. The fifteen per cent is arbitrary --- it was preferred to reusing a whole
neighbouring fold, which would have cost twenty per cent of the training lines.
\end{revisionblock}

\subsection{Response target}
\label{sec:target}

The target is \code{auc\_cc}, the area under the fitted dose-response curve as re-fitted by DrEval
\cite{dreval} from CTRPv2's raw measurements. Their reprocessing normalizes each replicate against its
own no-drug control and fits one curve with CurveCurator, folding replicate disagreement into the fit
rather than averaging replicates before fitting --- which their Methods argue is the practice that
avoids misleading response measures where replicates diverge sharply. A \emph{higher} value means a
\emph{less} sensitive cell line, and 1.0 is the no-effect level, because the fit pins the
low-concentration asymptote to the vehicle control \cite{ctrpv2}.

The same fit yields a second measure, \code{ln\_ic50\_cc}, the natural log of the IC50. It is not the
default and is used only where the two are compared, because it is undefined for roughly 40\% of curves
by construction: an IC50 falling well outside the measured dose range is discarded, which is the case
for most compounds that arrest rather than kill. Selecting on its availability would therefore be
selecting on potency. Each measure is written to its own targets file, so the two can be compared
without either overwriting the other. Implemented in
\code{scripts/preprocessing/ctrp\_to\_h5ad.py}; the source is pinned to one Zenodo record by
\code{scripts/layout.py}.

No winsorization and no quality filter are applied. The response is used as the source publishes it:
the benchmark this work follows applies no clipping either, in order to stay comparable with earlier
studies and to avoid discarding data.

\revision{\emph{Two earlier targets are not described here, deliberately (12.08.2026).} This subsection
previously defined \code{auc\_z}, a per-drug standardized target used from 13.07.2026 to 27.07.2026,
and argued for it at length. Both it and the \code{auc} measure it standardized have been removed from
the pipeline, and describing a retired target in Methods invites it to be read as part of the current
one. What survived the retirement is not a target at all but a finding about the objective --- that a
shared multi-task loss can be dominated by a few high-variance compounds --- and that is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, where it belongs. The full decomposition, the leak it closed, and the

A minority of cell-line--drug combinations were screened by CTRPv2 in two separate experiments. Their
two measurements are averaged into the single value used as the target, without weighting by replicate
count and without excluding pairs whose measurements disagree; where a combination was screened once,
that measurement is used as is.

The disagreement between repeated measurements is nonetheless
substantial and is reported here rather than left implicit, because it sets the scale against which any
correlation in Section~\ref{sec:results} should be read. Of the cell-line--drug pairs inside the
SCP542 intersection, \NRepPairs{} were screened twice, never more. Expressed relative to the spread of
that drug's response across cell lines --- which is precisely the quantity the model is asked to
predict --- the two measurements differ by a median of \RepDiffMed{} of one standard deviation, and
\RepDiffPct{}\% of repeated pairs differ by more than a full standard deviation. The median is used in
preference to the mean because a small number of extreme pairs would inflate the latter; it is the
middle pair, so half of the repeated measurements disagree by less than \RepDiffMed{} standard
deviations and half by more. A caveat limits how
far this generalizes: the repeats come from only \NRepLines{} cell lines, each re-screened against

\subsection{Evaluation}

Cell lines are evaluated out of fold. A 5-fold group $k$-fold split, grouped by cell line, is applied to
the 153 training and validation lines, so that no cell of a test line is seen during training. Each line
is predicted exactly once, the per-cell predictions of a line are averaged to the line level, and the
Spearman correlation between predicted and true values across the roughly \NIndep{} lines is computed per
drug and averaged over drugs. This protocol replaces an earlier fixed 27-line validation split, on which
the standard error of a correlation is approximately 0.2 and effects below 0.4 are not measurable. The
Pearson correlation tracks the Spearman value within 0.02 throughout.

For external comparison, the model is additionally evaluated with the DrEval package \cite{dreval}
(version 1.5.1), using its leave-cell-line-out splits, its baselines and its evaluation routine without
reimplementation. As recommended by the framework, metrics are reported after the mean drug and cell-line
effects have been removed from both the true and predicted responses, and they are pooled over all
cell-line-drug pairs rather than averaged per drug, so they are not directly comparable to the per-drug
correlations above.
\begin{revisionblock}
\textbf{Under this split design the cell-line effect is not in fact removed (added 12.08.2026).} The
sentence above describes the normalization as the framework defines it, and the implementation is
theirs unmodified --- but stating it that way overclaims what the metric controls for here, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} already says so. The baseline subtracted is
\code{overall mean + cell-line effect + drug effect}, fitted on the training folds. Under
leave-cell-line-out every scored line is by construction absent from those folds, so its effect
resolves to zero and the subtraction removes the \emph{drug} effect alone.

This is correct behaviour for a prediction benchmark --- no model can know an unseen line's effect ---
but it means a high normalized score is not evidence that a model has learned anything
drug-specific. Demonstrated rather than argued: a synthetic predictor emitting nothing but
\code{mean + line effect + drug effect}, with no drug-specific signal whatsoever, scores a normalized
Spearman correlation of \textbf{0.98} under this protocol, pooled and per drug alike. The question of
how much of a per-drug correlation is drug-specific biology rather than general cell-line fragility is
therefore left open by this metric, and answering it requires a diagnostic that reads held-out labels
