\section{Methods}

The pipeline ran from the two data sources through compound selection, target construction and the
two representations to per-cell training, evaluation and a multiple-instance model; each stage is a
numbered notebook under \code{notebooks/} that drives the code in \code{scripts/}
\cite{scp542,ctrpv2,dreval}. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows these stages in order, to give the overview
that the subsections then fill in, and the subsections follow the same order, each stating the
alternatives that were considered where a choice was made. The models built on one representation are
called its \emph{arm} --- the PCA arm and the scGPT arm --- and a \emph{configuration} is one
combination of loss and density weight (Section~\ref{sec:model}).

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}

All experiments used the intersection of the SCP542 atlas \cite{scp542} and CTRPv2
\cite{ctrpv2,ctrp_rees} as reprocessed by DrEval \cite{dreval}. Of the \NLinesAtlas{} cell lines in
the atlas, \NLinesRoster{} matched a name in the screen's roster, ten of these had never been screened,
and one further screened line was recovered by an explicit alias where the two sources spell it
differently, which left \NLines{} lines with response measurements and \NCells{} cells. Of CTRPv2's
\NDrugs{} compounds, \NDrugsModelled{} entered the target matrix; the other eleven had been screened
against fewer than \NCovMinLines{} of the overlapping lines. That cut kept output heads with almost no support out of
the shared model, but the threshold of \NCovMinLines{} was set rather than derived, follows no published convention
and was not varied, so its effect is unmeasured \cite{dreval}.

SCP542 is distributed after the source study's own quality control, and no cell-level filtering was
added here \cite{scp542}. Cells had been profiled in \NPools{} multiplexed pools on the 10x Chromium
platform \cite{zheng2017}, at about \SrcCellsPerLine{} cells per line, processed with Cell Ranger 3.0.1, and assigned
to lines by the consensus of a genotype-based and an expression-based method. Cells were retained when
their number of detected genes lay between \QcGenesLo{} and \QcGenesHi{} --- the floor removes droplets that
captured almost no RNA, the ceiling droplets holding two cells --- and lines with fewer than \QcMinCells{} cells
were removed, leaving an average of \SrcUmiMean{} unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) and \SrcGenesMean{} detected
genes per cell across \NGenes{} genes \cite{scp542,luecken2019}. A second, generic filter was not
applied, because the decisive doublet call rests on genotype, which the distributed matrix does not
carry; an expression-based pass would remove genuine cells at the margins without recovering that
signal \cite{scp542,luecken2019}. Two consequences were accepted: any statement about heterogeneity
concerns cells inside that window, which excludes states of low transcript content such as quiescent
cells near its floor; and the \NLines{} labelled lines, not the \NCtrpLines{} lines the screen covers,
bound the sample \cite{scp542,ctrpv2}.

The source study centres expression before its own analyses, and neither of its two forms was adopted
\cite{scp542}. Centring each gene across all cells is subsumed by the per-gene standardisation below.
Centring within each cell line removes the between-line variation, and since the label is constant
within a line, all of its signal is between lines; per-line centring would therefore remove exactly the
variance the target depends on. It is nonetheless the natural representation for the third research
question, and the point to revisit it is a model that pools a line's cells
(Section~\ref{sec:mil}).

Because the label is defined per cell line and compound, all cells of a line carry the same value and
are pseudo-replicates \cite{lazic2010}. The effective sample size was therefore taken to be the number of
cell lines inside the cross-validation (CV), \NLinesCV{}, not \NCells{} cells; treating cells as independent
would understate every uncertainty by roughly the square root of the number of cells per line
\cite{lazic2010,dreval}.

\subsection{Compound panel selection}
\label{sec:panel}

The compounds a multi-task model predicts determine what its scores mean, so the panel was selected
without reference to the response values the model is trained on \cite{dreval}. Two earlier panels of
this project had been selected on those values and were discarded: a gate requiring a compound to both
kill and spare cell lines excluded every cytostatic agent by construction, because a compound that
arrests rather than kills never crosses a viability threshold; and a literature panel inherited the same
dependency because its candidates had been pre-ranked on that statistic. Selecting on the spread of
one's own labels enriches for compounds that separate these particular lines, flatters every correlation
measured on them, and reads labels of lines that are later held out \cite{dreval,hafner}.

The panel was defined by three conditions, of which only the second used the screen. First, the compound
had to be approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for a cancer indication, according to
the enumeration of Sun et al.\ \cite{sun_fda}, which lists \NFdaDrugs{} drugs approved between 1949 and 2014.
Second, CTRPv2 had to have screened it against at least \PanelCovCut\,\% of the lines in the intersection
\cite{ctrpv2}; this is where the coverage distribution breaks --- \NFdaCovered{} candidates lie at or above it, the
lowest at \CovLowestIn\,\%, while the next compound reaches \CovNextOut\,\% and the tail falls to \CovTail\,\%. Third, a
published claim about the compound's response determinant had to exist and be verified against its
source, recorded per compound together with what the source establishes; the code asserted that every
such entry lay inside the set the first two conditions produce. Of the \NFdaDrugs{} drugs, \NFdaSmall{} are small
molecules (the remaining \NFdaBiologic{} are antibodies, enzymes, a cell therapy and a radiopharmaceutical, which a
viability screen cannot contain), \NFdaScreened{} were screened by CTRPv2, \NFdaCovered{} cleared the coverage cut and \NPanel{} carried
a verified claim: gemcitabine, doxorubicin, etoposide, paclitaxel and cisplatin among cytotoxics, and
imatinib, sorafenib, dasatinib, erlotinib, crizotinib and afatinib among targeted agents, at
\PanelCovLo--\PanelCovHi\,\% coverage.

Matching the approval list to the screen was not done on names, because approval lists use
international nonproprietary names, often of salts, while screens use development codes and free bases;
in addition, the two CTRPv2 files that carry annotation and responses name \NameMismatch{} of the \NDrugs{}
compounds differently, \NameMismatchFda{} of them approved or clinical \cite{ctrpv2,pubchem}. Four keys were therefore
tried and the successful one recorded per compound: both name spaces, the PubChem compound identifier,
and PubChem's parent-compound relation \cite{pubchem}, which links a salt to the dissolved free base and
alone accounted for \NParentMatch{} matches, among them imatinib and doxorubicin. The two internal files were joined
on the screen's own compound identifier, which matched all \NDrugs{}. Cisplatin required one explicit
correction: it is recorded under a non-standard name with a structure identifier for elemental
platinum, and was resolved on the screen's recorded structure and vendor entry.

Platinum agents were represented once. Cisplatin and carboplatin share their carrier ligand, form the
same adduct and are largely cross-resistant, so both would spend two of eleven positions on one
resistance mechanism; oxaliplatin's adduct evades mismatch repair and is complementary, but its
expression-level determinant is the least established of the three \cite{britten_ercc1,friboulet_ercc1}.
Cisplatin was chosen, and its determinant is recorded as contested rather than supporting: ERCC1
expression correlates with cisplatin resistance in cervical carcinoma lines \cite{britten_ercc1} but not
across twenty lung cancer lines \cite{shimizu_ercc1}, plausibly because only one of four ERCC1 isoforms
performs repair and assays cannot separate them \cite{friboulet_ercc1}. Gene-level features cannot
represent that isoform, so poor performance on cisplatin was anticipated by the literature. It was kept
rather than restricting the panel to settled determinants, which would bias the evaluation toward
compounds whose answer is already known. For the same reason the evidence for doxorubicin and
etoposide, both substrates of the ABCB1 efflux transporter, was recorded with its setting, and
topoisomerase expression was not claimed as a determinant, since it does not track sensitivity where it
was measured \cite{skinner_abcb1,omori_abcb1}.

The availability of a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) was excluded as a criterion: an IC50 is
undefined when a curve never crosses half-maximal viability, so its availability --- \PanelIcLo{} to \PanelIcHi{} lines
within this panel --- measures potency and would reintroduce the potency filter under the appearance of
a data-quality rule \cite{hafner,dreval}. The selection was implemented in
\code{notebooks/2\_drug\_selection.ipynb}, which wrote the panel and its citations to
\code{panel.csv} under \code{notebooks/outputs/panel/}; identifier resolution was in
\code{scripts/annotation/drug\_annotation.py} and \code{scripts/sources/pubchem.py}.

\subsection{Response target}
\label{sec:target}

The target was \code{auc\_cc}, the area under the dose--response curve (AUC) as re-fitted by DrEval
from CTRPv2's raw measurements with CurveCurator \cite{dreval,bayer2023curvecurator}. The order of
operations distinguishes it from CTRPv2's published AUC \cite{ctrpv2}: the standard procedure averages
the replicate plates, normalises the averages and fits one curve, whereas DrEval normalises each plate
to its own drug-free control wells and fits one curve through all of them. Since every plate has its own
baseline, averaging first mixes two baselines and divides by neither \cite{dreval}. A higher value means
a less sensitive line, and 1.0 is the no-effect level. DrEval's target was preferred to CTRPv2's for
three reasons: no normalisation was performed here, and the source was pinned to one Zenodo record
(\code{scripts/layout.py}), so the labels are version-fixed and re-derivable; it is defined for every
curve, whereas the IC50 from the same fit is defined for only about \IcDefinedPct\,\%, missing mostly for compounds
that arrest rather than kill; and it is the label the external benchmark scores against
\cite{dreval,bayer2023curvecurator}.

The response was used as published, without winsorisation or quality filter, as in the benchmark,
which applies no clipping in order to remain comparable with earlier studies \cite{dreval}. No per-compound
rescaling was applied either: where a compound's scale enters, it enters the objective as a weight
(Section~\ref{sec:model}), so the quantity predicted and reported remains the published value. Pairs
screened in more than one CTRPv2 experiment arrive as one curve fitted across their replicates; the
pipeline dropped the exact duplicates this produced and raised an error on any pair that was not identical
(\code{scripts/preprocessing/ctrp\_to\_h5ad.py}). Label quality was read from the fit statistics that
CurveCurator reports per curve, the coefficient of determination $R^2$ and the $p$-value of the fit
against a no-effect null \cite{bayer2023curvecurator}; a curve with $p > 0.05$ is called \emph{flat}
below.

\subsection{Representations}
\label{sec:representations}

SCP542 is distributed as counts per million (CPM), so library-size normalisation had already been
applied once, to the full matrix, and was not repeated \cite{scp542}. For PCA, expression was transformed
as
\begin{equation}
  E_{ij} = \log_2\!\left(1 + \mathrm{CPM}_{ij}/10\right),
  \label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
the quantification of the dataset's authors, who divide by ten because fewer than $10^5$ UMIs are
detected per cell \cite{scp542}. A logarithm is needed because PCA weighs each gene by its absolute
spread, so on the raw scale abundant housekeeping genes would dominate the leading components, whereas
expression differences are multiplicative and the logarithm makes a fold change the same distance at any
abundance \cite{luecken2019}. The \NHvg{} highly variable genes (HVGs) were selected on this matrix by
the dispersion-based Seurat criterion \cite{satija2015,wolf2018scanpy}; each gene was standardised to
zero mean and unit variance with $z$-scores clipped at \CfgZClip, and \NPca{} principal components were kept
\cite{jolliffe2016} (\code{scripts/preprocessing/add\_pca.py}). The number \NPca{} was chosen to match
scGPT's embedding width, so that the comparison is at equal width; it is not a statement about the data.

scGPT is a transformer that reads a cell as a set of gene tokens with binned expression values and was
pretrained on roughly 33 million human cells to predict masked expression values from the rest of the
cell; the output at a dedicated cell token serves as a \NPca-dimensional, length-normalised cell
embedding \cite{scgpt}. The pretrained whole-human weights were used frozen, through the embedding entry
point \code{embed\_data} (\code{scripts/preprocessing/gen\_embeds.py}). On that path the only operation
applied to expression was a binning of each cell into \CfgBins{} bins at quantiles of that cell's own non-zero
values, and the model read at most \CfgMaxGenes{} genes per cell, drawn at random from its non-zero genes
\cite{scgpt}. scGPT therefore received the same CPM matrix without logarithm or standardisation. This
asymmetry is deliberate: binning is a rank transform within a cell, so any monotone per-cell
transformation --- library-size division, logarithm, or both --- leaves every bin unchanged, and applying
the PCA preprocessing to both arms would alter nothing scGPT sees while disguising that the two consume
different information \cite{scgpt,luecken2019}. It also means the choice of source matrix is open only for
PCA: substituting the pre-quality-control UMI counts would change gene selection and the components but
not the scGPT input.

scGPT matches genes to its vocabulary by symbol, and the expression matrix carries an older annotation of
the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC) than the vocabulary \cite{hgnc}. Renamed genes would therefore
have been discarded although the vocabulary holds them under their current name: \NRescuedHvg{} genes of
the \NHvg-gene variant and \NRescuedAll{} of the full-transcriptome variant, the latter carrying
\PctRescuedAll\,\% of each cell's transcriptome, and because of the per-cell binning every remaining gene
would have shifted bin as well. The conversion step therefore recorded each gene's current HGNC symbol,
and the embedding step used it where the distributed symbol is not in the vocabulary, which raised the
\NHvg-gene variant from \NVocabDirect{} to \NVocab{} embedded genes (\code{scripts/annotation/gene\_symbols.py}).

The \NHvg-gene set was used throughout. A sweep over five gene-set sizes from 1{,}000 genes to the full
transcriptome, all embedded with the same code, moved the PCA arm by \SweepRangePca{} compounds beating their per-compound
baseline out of \NDrugsModelled{}, against a fold-to-fold standard deviation (SD) of \SweepFoldSd{}, and was flat
within error for both representations (\code{notebooks/analysis/qc/verify\_variants.ipynb}, \S9)
\cite{scgpt}. For scGPT the sweep compared selection rather than count: at \NHvg{} genes it read about
\GenesSeenHvg{} dispersion-selected genes per cell, in the full-transcriptome variant about \GenesSeenAll{} drawn at random
under its cap, so the flat result shows only that twice as many random genes do no better than selected
ones, not that scGPT gains nothing from the full transcriptome.

Three quantities were estimated from data before training --- the HVG selection, the per-gene
standardisation and the PCA rotation --- and none sees a label, but they were decided together because
they pose one question: how much a fit may see \cite{luecken2019,dreval}. Gene selection used every
cell. Selecting per fold would have produced five gene sets, so comparisons across folds,
representations and the gene-set sweep would differ in genes as well as cells; the lines without
labels, about a ninth of the atlas, were kept in it, since they carry no label to leak, improve the
variance estimates, and both arms receive the identical set. The standardisation and rotation used by
the cross-validated models were fitted per fold, on the fold's fitting cells only --- its training lines
less the lines withheld for early stopping --- and applied to its held-out lines. This was the one place
the arms differ in what is fitted on the data, so its fitting set was restricted to the cells the model
itself is fitted on: the whole training side would have given PCA about \CfgExtraPct\,\% more cells than the
model it feeds, at a cost of about \CfgHoldoutPct\,\% of the cells available to the decomposition. Storing five
fold-specific matrices was rejected, at about a gigabyte and because it would tie the fold design to the
preprocessing; the fits were cached per run, keyed by the reproducible fold assignment, in single
precision like the all-cells decomposition, which was kept for description only. Two asymmetries
remained: PCA decomposes every selected gene while scGPT reads only those in its vocabulary, and PCA is
fitted on this atlas while scGPT is frozen, so the comparison is not like-for-like with respect to
adaptation to the data.

\subsection{Model and training}
\label{sec:model}

Both representations were fed into the same multilayer perceptron (MLP): input dropout at rate \CfgInDrop{}
\cite{srivastava2014}, a shared trunk of two blocks of linear layer, layer normalisation
\cite{ba2016layernorm}, Gaussian error linear unit (GELU) activation \cite{hendrycks2016gelu} and dropout at \CfgDrop{}, with widths \CfgHidA{} and
\CfgHidB{}, and one linear output head per compound, $K = \NPanel$ (\code{scripts/model/OncoMLP.py}). A shared
trunk with $K$ heads was preferred to $K$ separate models because what a cell's representation must
encode is largely shared across compounds, and separate models would relearn it from about \NLinesCV{} labels
each \cite{scgpt}. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows this network for one cell.

fine-tuned) passes through a shared trunk of two hidden blocks (\CfgHidA{} and \CfgHidB{} units; each

\NPanel{} linear heads, one per compound, which together form a single linear layer over the trunk. Right:
the out-of-fold predicted AUC of one held-out line (SKES1, bone) per compound as bars, with the measured
CTRPv2 value as diamonds; low AUC means sensitive, high means resistant, and the bar shade follows the
predicted value. Cisplatin is labelled by its CTRPv2 name, \code{platin}. Generated by
\code{docs/make\_figures.py}.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}
 The objective was a masked, optionally weighted error averaged over screened pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \LossObjective
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $n$ indexes cells, $j$ compounds, and
\begin{equation}
  \LossWeightMatrix
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
combines the mask $M_{nj}$, which is $1$ where the cell's line was screened against compound $j$ and $0$
otherwise, with a density weight $w_j$. Unscreened pairs therefore enter neither numerator nor
denominator, which matters because the target matrix is \TargetDensity\,\% dense \cite{dreval}. The error $\ell$
was either squared or absolute, giving a mean squared error (MSE) or mean absolute error (MAE) objective.

The density weight up-weights rare response values so that the sparse extremes are not dominated by the
crowded middle of a compound's distribution \cite{dir,denseweight}:
\begin{equation}
  \LossWeightFn
  \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
where $\hat p_j$ is a Gaussian kernel density estimate with Scott's bandwidth \cite{scott1992}, fitted per
compound on the line-level labels $y_{ij}$ of the fold's training lines $\mathcal{I}_j$, the weights are
clipped to $[1/c, c]$ and normalised to mean one. It was fitted per compound because the metric is
within-compound, and on lines rather than cells because a line with \CellsMax{} cells would otherwise bend
the density while one with \CellsMin{} barely registered. The cap $c = \CfgCap$ is a safety limit against a few extreme
lines driving a fit over about \NLinesCV{} points; it is arbitrary and was held fixed, since sweeping it with
$\alpha$ would confound the two. The exponent was not justified in advance but swept,
$\alpha \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$, where $\alpha = 0$ gives $w_j \equiv 1$
(\code{scripts/training/density\_weighting.py}) \cite{denseweight}. Two losses and three exponents gave six
configurations per representation.

The response is not centred on zero, and its centre differs per compound, from \PanelMeanLo{} to
\PanelMeanHi{} across the panel. A freshly initialised head predicts near zero and would spend its first
epochs climbing to those levels, so that early stopping could select a checkpoint before any between-line
structure was fitted. Each head's bias was therefore initialised at its compound's mean over the fold's
fitting lines, standard practice for a base rate \cite{lin2017focal}; the weights remained random, so the
model did not start as a constant predictor.

The optimiser was AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay $0$,
batch size \CfgBatch{} cells, at most \CfgEpochs{} epochs, a halving of the learning rate after \CfgSchedPatience{} epochs without
improvement, and gradient clipping at norm \CfgClip \cite{pascanu2013} (\code{scripts/training/training\_utils.py}).
Weight decay was zero because the inherited value had been chosen for Adam, which couples decay into the
adaptive gradient, whereas AdamW applies it to the parameters directly, so the same coefficient means
different things and no sourced value existed \cite{loshchilov2019adamw}; biases and normalisation
parameters were nonetheless excluded from decay, following the \code{transformers} convention
\cite{wolf2020transformers}, so that the question can later be settled by one coefficient. Early stopping
with patience \CfgPatience{} \cite{prechelt1998} restored the checkpoint of lowest error on an inner hold-out of
\CfgHoldoutPct\,\% of each fold's training lines, grouped by line (\code{scripts/training/cv.py}); measuring it on the
scored fold would have made each score a minimum over epochs on its own evaluation data. The per-compound
means and head initialisation used the remaining fitting lines. This three-way split follows the DrEval
benchmark \cite{dreval}; the \CfgHoldoutPct\,\% is arbitrary and was preferred to reusing a whole fold, which would
have cost \CfgFoldPct\,\% of the training lines. Every configuration was trained under seeds 42, 43 and 44, and no
hyperparameter was tuned for either representation. Training ran on Apple's Metal Performance Shaders
(MPS) backend.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Cell lines were split about 70/15/15 into training, validation and test, so that all cells of a line
fall on one side, and the assignment was frozen to a versioned file (\code{splits/split\_ctrp.csv},
\NLinesFit/\NLinesVal/\NLinesTest{} lines), because a redraw after any change to the label filters would silently move lines
between parts \cite{dreval}. The \NLinesTest{} test lines were never used. The \NLinesCV{} training and validation
lines entered a five-fold CV grouped by cell line, in which each line was predicted
exactly once. The per-cell predictions of a line were averaged, and the score was the Spearman
correlation \cite{spearman1904} between predicted and true line-level responses within each compound,
over the held-out lines pooled across folds, averaged over compounds and seeds:
\begin{equation}
  \bar\rho = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{s=1}^{3}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}
  \rho_{\mathrm{S}}\!\left(\hat y^{(s)}_{\cdot j},\, y_{\cdot j}\right).
  \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
The correlation is within a compound because pooling all pairs is dominated by differences in potency
between compounds: pooled, the correlation is about \SpPooled{}, and a predictor knowing only each
compound's average response reaches \DrDrugMean{} \cite{dreval}. Line level and pooled folds were chosen
over the alternatives because one point per line is one point per label, whereas cell-level scoring adds
about \CellsPerLineMean{} points per label without independent information and weights lines by their cell count, which
varies 36-fold; and per-fold correlations over about 30 lines each are biased upward, which was
visible in every configuration \cite{spearman1904}. The
alternatives --- cell level, and per-fold averaging --- were computed for comparison
(\code{panel\_aggregation\_comparison.csv}).

The loss and weighting configurations were compared under a decision rule fixed before the run
(\code{notebooks/5\_evaluation.ipynb} \S1.3). Four quantities were computed per compound and averaged:
\emph{order}, the within-compound Spearman correlation; \emph{top of order}, the share of the \CfgTopK{} most
sensitive lines that the model also ranks in its top \CfgTopK; \emph{values}, the MAE in the target's units;
and \emph{spread}, the slope of a regression of truth on prediction, which is 1 for a calibrated model
\cite{vancalster2016}. A challenger displaced the incumbent only if it was better on order and worse on
no other quantity by more than that quantity's own seed band, the half-range over the three seeds. The
incumbent was the unweighted MAE configuration on PCA, after the initially designated unweighted MSE
configuration proved the only one not reproducible across executions.

Three controls were computed on the same folds. A ridge regression \cite{hoerl1970} on cell-line means
averaged each line's cells into one \NPca-dimensional vector and fitted one model per compound and fold
on the fitting lines, with the same per-fold PCA, choosing its penalty from \CfgGridN{} log-spaced values between
$10^{-2}$ and $10^{6}$ by efficient leave-one-out error \cite{hastie2009,pedregosa2011}; features entered
unscaled, since a log-spaced grid is invariant to feature scale up to a shift. The grid was widened from
\CfgGridOldN{} values ending at $10^{4}$ after a test fixed before the run --- whether any selected penalty lies on a
grid end --- fired in \RidgeEdgePca{} of 55 PCA fits and \RidgeEdgeScgpt{} of 55 scGPT fits, all at the ceiling
(\code{scripts/evaluation/ridge\_grid\_refit.py}). Second, a model with all \NDrugsModelled{} heads was
scored on every compound against a per-compound constant, to test the panel against the unselected catalogue
(\code{notebooks/4a\_percell\_training.ipynb} \S D). Third, the model was evaluated with DrEval 1.5.1
\cite{dreval} in its leave-cell-line-out (LCO) mode, using its splits, its naive baselines, its
per-compound random forest \cite{breiman2001} and elastic net, and its normalised metric, unmodified. That
metric subtracts a fitted overall mean, cell-line effect and compound effect from truth and prediction; under
LCO a scored line is absent from training, so its effect is zero and only the compound effect is removed.
DrEval's leave-pairs-out (LPO) mode, in which held-out pairs belong to lines seen in training, was run
for its baselines to measure how much a line's average response alone predicts
(\code{scripts/evaluation/dreval\_lpo.py}).

Since LCO cannot remove the cell-line effect, its share was measured directly on held-out labels. A
line's fragility $f_i$ was defined as its mean response over the other ten panel compounds, so that the
compound being scored never enters its own proxy, and for each compound the rank-partial correlation
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{yp\cdot f} = \frac{\rho_{yp} - \rho_{yf}\,\rho_{pf}}{\sqrt{\left(1-\rho_{yf}^2\right)\left(1-\rho_{pf}^2\right)}}
  \label{eq:partial}
\end{equation}
was computed between truth $y$ and prediction $p$ with fragility $f$ controlled out, all quantities
ranked \cite{spearman1904,dreval} (\code{scripts/evaluation/bias\_accounting.py}).

Four alternative explanations of a difference between the representations were tested on the same
folds (\code{notebooks/4a\_percell\_training.ipynb} \S C, \S E; \code{scripts/evaluation/}): label supply,
by thinning the labelled lines per fold to 25, 50, 75 and all 103 with input, architecture and loss
fixed; capacity, by replacing the trunk with a single linear head; input scale, by rescaling the scGPT
embedding to the magnitude of the PCA input and recording the epoch of best validation error
(\code{input\_scale\_test.py}); and fragility, by Equation~\ref{eq:partial}. The input-dropout rate was
swept over $\{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ (\code{input\_dropout\_test.py}), and label quality was
related to the per-compound score by a Spearman correlation between the share of flat curves and the
score over the \NPanel{} compounds \cite{bayer2023curvecurator}.

\subsection{Multiple-instance learning and the heterogeneity criterion}
\label{sec:mil}

The per-cell objective cannot answer the third research question, because it penalises within-line
variation exactly. For one compound and one line with per-cell predictions $p_c$, mean $\bar p$ and label
$y$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{c=1}^{n}\left(p_c - y\right)^2
  \;=\; \underbrace{\left(\bar{p} - y\right)^2}_{\text{bag loss}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_c(p)}_{\text{penalty on within-line spread}},
  \label{eq:mil-decomposition}
\end{equation}
an identity, since $\sum_c (p_c - \bar p) = 0$ removes the cross term. The per-cell loss, regrouped by
line, is the left-hand side; a mean-pooled multiple-instance learning (MIL) model \cite{dietterich1997}
minimises only the first term. The regrouping is valid because the loss is a sum over cells, and the
density weight, a function of the label, is constant within a line. No hyperparameter reaches the second
term; only removing it does. The architecture and the criterion below were fixed on 13.08.2026, before
the model was run (\code{notebooks/4b\_mil\_training.ipynb}, \S2).

Of the two standard MIL formulations \cite{ilse2018}, the instance-level one, in which every cell keeps
its own prediction and these are aggregated, was chosen over embedding-level attention pooling, which
usually predicts better: the question is whether individual cells receive different predictions, and only
the instance-level form produces that quantity. A bag was one complete cell line --- every sequenced cell,
not a sample --- because the label is defined per line, and because for a sub-bag of $B$ cells drawn from
a line of $n$ the expected loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{E}\,\mathcal{L}_B = \left(\bar p_n - y\right)^2 + \frac{\sigma^2}{B}\left(1 - \frac{B-1}{n-1}\right),
  \label{eq:subbag}
\end{equation}
which is the per-cell loss at $B = 1$ and contains no variance term only at $B = n$; the bag size
therefore sets how strongly the objective charges for the quantity under study. Complete bags also make
each line one training example, removing the per-cell objective's weighting of lines by their cell count,
which ranges from \CellsMin{} to \CellsMax{}, at the cost of one gradient step per line. The bag prediction was the
mean of its cells' predictions, matching the label's definition. A variance term in the loss was rejected
because it would make the spread criterion pass by construction and change objective and architecture at
once; a maximum or quantile aggregator was rejected because it does not force variation either and does
not match an averaged label \cite{ilse2018}. Mean pooling can be minimised by collapsing a bag onto its
mean, which is why the criterion leads with a positive control; had the control failed, the aggregator
was not to be exchanged for one that passes.

The criterion had five stages, each passing against a null or a comparison rather than an invented
threshold. The \emph{input ceiling} measured the within-line share of variance $w$ of each representation
before training and failed only on collapse; its between-to-within ratio $(1-w)/w$ is reported. The
\emph{positive control} mixed the cells of two lines whose response differs, taken from the lowest
quartile of a compound's pairwise response differences, and measured the area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve (AUROC) separating the two populations \emph{within} one bag --- the
Mann--Whitney statistic divided by $n_A n_B$ \cite{mannwhitney1947} --- against a null from permuting the
source labels within the bag. It was scored inside the bag because a bag-level score is reproduced
exactly by a model that gives all cells the same value. The \emph{spread} stage required the within-line
SD of MIL predictions to exceed that of the per-cell model, without margin, since
Equation~\ref{eq:mil-decomposition} makes this a test of the deleted term. The \emph{reproducibility}
stage correlated per-cell predictions across seeds against a null permuting cells within each line.
The \emph{confound} stage regressed the predictions within each line on total UMI count, detected genes,
mitochondrial fraction and the dataset's G1/S and G2/M scores \cite{scp542}, and vetoed a pass if the
adjusted $R^2$ reached the squared cross-seed correlation, both being shares of the same variance.

The adjusted $R^2$ was used because the unadjusted one is biased upward by the number of covariates by an
amount that shrinks with the number of cells, so one bar would differ between a line of \CellsMin{} cells and one
of \CellsMax{}; on simulated predictions unrelated to the covariates the unadjusted value read about 19\,\%
and the adjusted about $-1\,\%$. Depth and mitochondrial fraction cannot be computed from the CPM matrix,
from which library size has been divided out and in which only four of 13 mitochondrial genes are
HVGs, so both were computed from the study's unnormalised UMI matrix over all genes: a total over HVGs
alone is depth multiplied by a biological share, and a confounder carrying the signal under test could
veto a true positive \cite{scp542,luecken2019}. The criterion can establish whether per-cell predictions
vary within a line reproducibly and not as a sequencing artefact; whether that variation is
drug-response heterogeneity cannot be established, because no per-cell response was measured.
