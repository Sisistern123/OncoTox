\section{Data and preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}

All experiments used the intersection of the SCP542 atlas \cite{scp542} and CTRPv2
\cite{ctrpv2,ctrp_rees} as reprocessed by DrEval \cite{dreval}. Of the \NLinesAtlas{} cell lines in
the atlas, \NLinesRoster{} matched a name in the screen's roster, ten of these had never been screened,
and one further screened line was recovered by an explicit alias where the two sources spell it
differently, which left \NLines{} lines with response measurements and \NCellsLabelled{} cells from \NCancerTypes{} cancer
types. Of the
\NDrugs{} compounds in DrEval's CTRPv2 release, \NDrugsModelled{} entered the target matrix. The other eleven were excluded because
their response had been measured in fewer than \NCovMinLines{} of the overlapping cell lines, an arbitrary
threshold chosen to ensure sufficient coverage for training. The data sources and the code are listed in
Appendix~\ref{sec:availability}.

SCP542 is distributed after the source study's own quality control, and no cell-level filtering was
added here \cite{scp542}. Any statement about heterogeneity therefore concerns cells inside the source
study's detected-gene window (Appendix~\ref{app:qc}). The metadata also carry per-cell scores for the
12 recurrent heterogeneity programs of the source study, of which eleven are distinct
(Appendix~\ref{app:qc}).

A test set of \NLinesTest{} cell lines was set aside once and kept fixed throughout, with all cells of a
line on the same side.
% (\code{splits/split\_ctrp.csv}, written by \code{scripts/preprocessing/create\_splits.py})
It was reserved for a final evaluation of a chosen configuration, which was not performed within this
project. The remaining \NLinesCV{} lines were
divided into five cross-validation (CV) folds grouped by cell line (leave-cell-line-out, LCO), so that each line is held out exactly
once. Within each fold, \CfgHoldoutPct\,\% of the training lines were withheld for early stopping
(Section~\ref{sec:model}), and the remaining lines, about a hundred per fold, are referred to as the
fitting lines.

Because the label is defined per cell line and compound, all cells of a line carry the same value and
are pseudo-replicates \cite{lazic2010}. The effective sample size was therefore taken to be the number of
cell lines in the CV, \NLinesCV{}, not \NCellsCV{} cells \cite{lazic2010,dreval}.

\section{Compound panel selection}
\label{sec:panel}

Because the reported score is an average over the panel's compounds, the panel was selected without
reference to the response values the model is trained on (Section~\ref{sec:rd-q1}). A compound entered
the panel if it met three conditions: (i) approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for a
cancer indication \cite{sun_fda}, (ii) screening by CTRPv2 against at least \PanelCovCut\,\% of the
overlapping cell lines \cite{ctrpv2}, and (iii) being well characterised in the literature. Condition
(iii) served only to keep this initial panel small, so that the approach could be tested on a subset of
well-known compounds before being extended to the full screen. Condition (ii) used CTRPv2 only to count
the cell lines on which a compound had been tested, never the measured responses.

Of the FDA-approved drugs, \NFdaScreened{} were screened by CTRPv2, \NFdaCovered{} cleared the coverage
cut and \NPanel{} were selected: gemcitabine \cite{ctrp_rees}, doxorubicin \cite{skinner_abcb1},
etoposide \cite{omori_abcb1,skinner_abcb1}, paclitaxel \cite{ctrpv2,skinner_abcb1} and cisplatin
\cite{britten_ercc1,shimizu_ercc1,friboulet_ercc1} among cytotoxics, and imatinib
\cite{ctrp_rees,skinner_abcb1}, sorafenib \cite{scp542}, dasatinib \cite{ctrpv2}, erlotinib
\cite{scdeal,pao2004}, crizotinib \cite{ctrpv2} and afatinib \cite{ctrpv2} among targeted agents, at \PanelCovLo--\PanelCovHi\,\% coverage. The \PanelCovCut\,\% cut lies where the coverage
distribution breaks: the lowest included compound reaches \CovLowestIn\,\%, the next \CovNextOut\,\%, and
the tail falls to \CovTail\,\%.

Approval list and screen were matched on compound identifiers rather than names, and every one of
the \NDrugs{} compounds was resolved (Appendix~\ref{app:ids}).

Of the platinum agents, only cisplatin was included, since cisplatin and carboplatin share a mechanism
of action and are fully cross-resistant \cite{rabik2007}.

\section{Response target}
\label{sec:target}

The target was the AUC (Section~\ref{sec:bg-screens}) as refitted by DrEval with CurveCurator
\cite{dreval,bayer2023curvecurator} (Appendix~\ref{app:labels}). DrEval's refitted values were used here as published, without any
further processing. A higher value means
a less sensitive line, and 1.0 is the no-effect level. The median over all screened pairs of the
\NDrugsModelled{} compounds was \AucMedian{}, so the typical pair barely responds. The AUC was used mainly for its coverage: it is defined for every
curve, whereas the IC50 from the same fit is defined for only about \IcDefinedPct\,\%, missing mostly for compounds
that arrest rather than kill \cite{dreval,bayer2023curvecurator}.

No per-compound rescaling was applied: where a compound's scale matters, it enters the objective as a
weight (Section~\ref{sec:model}), so the model predicts and is scored on the AUC in its original units.
Label quality was assessed with two fit statistics that CurveCurator reports per curve: the $p$-value of
the fit against a no-effect null \cite{bayer2023curvecurator} and the coefficient of determination $R^2$.
A curve with $p > 0.05$ is called \emph{flat} below. This threshold is the report's own, whereas
CurveCurator's classification of curves also uses the effect size.

\section{Representations}
\label{sec:representations}

SCP542 is distributed as CPM \cite{scp542_portal}, so library-size normalisation
had already been applied once, to the full matrix, and was not repeated. For PCA, expression was transformed
as
\begin{equation}
  E_{ij} = \log_2\!\left(1 + \mathrm{CPM}_{ij}/10\right),
  \label{eq:log}
\end{equation}
the quantification of the dataset's authors, who divide by ten because on average fewer than $10^5$
UMIs are detected per cell \cite{scp542}. The logarithm is the standard transformation for such data (Section~\ref{sec:bg-representation}).
The \NHvg{} HVGs were selected on this matrix by
the dispersion-based Seurat criterion (Section~\ref{sec:bg-representation}) \cite{satija2015,wolf2018scanpy}. Within each CV fold, each gene was standardised to zero mean and unit variance using its mean and
standard deviation over the fold's training cells, excluding the lines withheld for early stopping
(Section~\ref{sec:model}), with $z$-scores clipped at \CfgZClip. The principal components were computed on
these training cells, and all cells of the fold, including its held-out lines, were projected onto the
first \NPca{} of them \cite{jolliffe2016}. Unlike some analyses of the source study \cite{scp542}, expression was not centred within each cell
line: the label is constant within a line, so per-line centring would remove the between-line variance
it depends on. The number of components was set to scGPT's embedding width, for a comparison at equal
width, rather than derived from the data.
% (\code{scripts/preprocessing/add\_pca.py})

scGPT (Section~\ref{sec:bg-foundation}) was used through its cell embedding, the output at a dedicated
cell token, which is \NPca-dimensional \cite{scgpt} and which the embedding routine normalises to unit length \cite{scgpt_code}. Cells were embedded with the cell-embedding routine distributed with scGPT, with
the pretrained whole-human weights frozen.
% entry point \code{embed\_data} (\code{scripts/preprocessing/gen\_embeds.py})
scGPT received the same CPM matrix without logarithm or standardisation, since its per-cell binning
makes it invariant to both (Appendix~\ref{app:scgpt-input}).

Genes renamed since the expression matrix was annotated were matched to the scGPT vocabulary under
their current symbols (Appendix~\ref{app:hgnc}).
% (\code{scripts/annotation/gene\_symbols.py})

The \NHvg-gene set was used throughout. Its size was checked by a sweep over five gene-set sizes from
1{,}000 genes to the full transcriptome, all embedded with the same code (Section~\ref{sec:results-q2}).

No step before training used the response labels, and held-out lines influenced neither the model nor
their own PCA features. The only step that used all cells was the selection of the HVGs, which uses
expression alone and from which both representations draw their input (Section~\ref{sec:rd-q2}). The
descriptive input ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:mil}, which feeds no model, used one PCA fitted to the cells
of all \NLinesCV{} CV lines.

\section{Model and training}
\label{sec:model}

Each training example was a single cell: its \NPca-dimensional representation as input and its line's
response to each panel compound as target. Both representations were fed into the same multilayer perceptron (MLP) architecture, trained
separately for each: input dropout at rate \CfgInDrop{}
\cite{srivastava2014}, a shared trunk of two blocks of linear layer, layer normalisation
\cite{ba2016layernorm}, Gaussian error linear unit (GELU) activation \cite{hendrycks2016gelu} and dropout at \CfgDrop{}, with widths \CfgHidA{} and
\CfgHidB{}, and one linear output head per compound, $K = \NPanel$. The network was kept small so that it could
model non-linear relations between expression and response without overfitting the roughly \NLinesCV{}
labelled lines.
A shared trunk with $K$ heads was preferred to $K$ separate models so that the model learns from how each
cell line responds across all compounds: the trunk is trained on the labels of every compound, so what it
learns from a line's response to one compound can inform its predictions for the others
\cite{partin2023}. Separate models would each learn from only about a hundred labels per fold. Whether the trunk's
non-linearity and shared representation are used was tested by replacing it with a single linear layer,
with \NPanel{} and with \NDrugsModelled{} heads (Section~\ref{sec:results-q2}, Table~\ref{tab:capacity}).
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows this network for one cell.
% (\code{scripts/model/OncoMLP.py})

 The objective was a masked, optionally weighted error averaged over screened pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \LossObjective
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $n$ indexes cells, $j$ compounds, and
\begin{equation}
  \LossWeightMatrix
  \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
combines the mask $M_{nj}$, which is $1$ where the cell's line was screened against compound $j$ and $0$
otherwise, with a density weight $w_j$. Unscreened pairs therefore enter neither numerator nor
denominator. Over the CV lines, \PanelDensity\,\% of the (line, compound) pairs of the panel were
screened. The error $\ell$
was either squared or absolute, giving a mean squared error (MSE) or mean absolute error (MAE) objective.

The density weight up-weights rare response values so that the sparse extremes are not dominated by the
crowded middle of a compound's distribution \cite{dir,denseweight}:
\begin{equation}
  \LossWeightFn
  \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
where $\hat p_j$ is a density estimate of compound $j$'s labels $y_{ij}$ over the fold's fitting lines
$i \in \mathcal{I}_j$, and $c = \CfgCap$ (Appendix~\ref{app:density}). The exponent was varied over $\alpha \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$:
$\alpha = 0$ gives unweighted training, and $\alpha = 0.5$ and $\alpha = 1$ correspond to the square-root
inverse and inverse weightings of \cite{dir}. The choices behind these settings are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:density}. Two losses and three exponents gave six
configurations per representation.
% (\code{scripts/training/density\_weighting.py})

Training used AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} with early stopping \cite{prechelt1998} on an inner
hold-out of each fold's training lines, grouped by line, since measuring it on the scored fold would
have made each score a minimum over epochs on its own evaluation data. Each head's bias was initialised at its compound's mean over the fold's fitting lines
(Appendix~\ref{app:training}). Every configuration was trained under three seeds, and no hyperparameter
was tuned for either representation (Table~\ref{tab:training}).

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The per-cell predictions of each held-out line were averaged, and the score was the Spearman
correlation \cite{spearman1904} between predicted and true line-level responses within each compound,
over the held-out lines screened against it, pooled across folds, averaged over compounds and seeds:
\begin{equation}
  \bar\rho = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{s=1}^{3}\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}
  \rho_{\mathrm{S}}\!\left(\hat y^{(s)}_{\cdot j},\, y_{\cdot j}\right).
  \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
The correlation is within a compound because pooling all pairs is dominated by differences in potency
between compounds (Section~\ref{sec:bg-prediction}) \cite{dreval}. It is computed on lines because one
point per line is one point per label, and on the held-out lines of all folds pooled, the more
conservative of the two options. The alternatives were computed for comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:results-q1}).
% (\code{panel\_aggregation\_comparison.csv})

Whether a different loss or the density weighting improves on the default, the unweighted MAE
configuration on PCA, was decided by a pre-specified rule: a configuration counted as an
improvement only if it ranked lines better and was not clearly worse on absolute error, calibration or
the recovery of the most sensitive lines (Appendix~\ref{app:decision}).
% (\code{notebooks/5\_evaluation.ipynb} \S1.3)
The rule therefore need not select the configuration with the highest order score, which is reported as
the highest-scoring configuration (Section~\ref{sec:results-q1}).

Three controls were computed. The first two used the same folds, and the third DrEval's own splits.
% (until 21.10.2026: "Three controls were computed on the same folds", which the DrEval control was not)
First, a ridge regression \cite{hoerl1970} on cell-line means
averaged each line's cells into one \NPca-dimensional vector and fitted one model per compound and fold
on the fitting lines, on each representation and for PCA with the same per-fold PCA, choosing its penalty by leave-one-out error
(Appendix~\ref{app:controls}). Second, the model was trained with all \NDrugsModelled{} heads, once with the
trunk and once with a single linear layer in its place, and scored on the panel compounds and on every
compound against a per-compound constant. This tests the panel against the unselected catalogue, and
whether training on more compounds helps the trunk.
% (\code{notebooks/4a\_percell\_training.ipynb} \S D)
Third, the model was evaluated with DrEval \cite{dreval} under its LCO protocol, with its splits, its
naive baselines --- predicting the global mean, the compound mean, the cell-line mean, or both mean
effects together --- its per-compound random forest \cite{breiman2001}, and its
normalised metric, unmodified (Appendix~\ref{app:controls}). DrEval's
leave-pairs-out (LPO) protocol, in which held-out pairs belong to lines seen in training, was run for its
baselines, which there include the tissue mean, to measure how much a line's average response alone
predicts. Because a held-out line is absent from
training, the LCO metric can remove only the compound effect, not the cell-line effect.
Under LCO the normalised metric is therefore zero for every naive baseline by construction, up to
fold-to-fold noise, so a positive score is signal beyond them.
% (\code{dreval/dreval\_lco\_results.csv}: compound-mean and mean-effects 0.0000; global-mean and
% cell-line-mean 0.0063, fold SD 0.112. Moved here from Results on 21.10.2026, where it read "DrEval's
% naive baselines score zero on that metric \cite{dreval}".)
% DrEval version 1.5.1 (\code{scripts/evaluation/dreval\_lpo.py})

The share of the cell-line effect was therefore measured directly on held-out labels. As a proxy for a line's general sensitivity, its fragility $f_i$ was defined here as its mean
response over the other ten panel compounds, so that the compound being scored never enters its own
proxy. For each compound the
partial correlation
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{yp\cdot f} = \frac{\rho_{yp} - \rho_{yf}\,\rho_{pf}}{\sqrt{\left(1-\rho_{yf}^2\right)\left(1-\rho_{pf}^2\right)}}
  \label{eq:partial}
\end{equation}
was computed between truth $y$ and prediction $p$ with fragility $f$ controlled out, all quantities
ranked: the standard first-order partial correlation applied to Spearman correlations
\cite{spearman1904}.
% (\code{scripts/evaluation/bias\_accounting.py})

Five alternative explanations of a difference between the representations were tested: label supply,
atlas size, capacity, input scale and fragility, each described with its result
(Section~\ref{sec:rd-q2}). All but the atlas-size test used the same folds, since removing whole lines
re-partitions the remaining ones. The input-dropout rate was swept over
$\{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$, and label quality was related to the per-compound score by a
Spearman correlation between the share of flat curves and the score over the \NPanel{} compounds
\cite{bayer2023curvecurator}.

\section{Multiple-instance learning and its evaluation}
\label{sec:mil}

The per-cell objective is unsuited to the third research question, because it penalises variation
within a line. For one compound and one line of $n$ cells with per-cell predictions $p_m$, mean $\bar p$ and label $y$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\left(p_m - y\right)^2
  \;=\; \underbrace{\left(\bar{p} - y\right)^2}_{\text{bag loss}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_m(p)}_{\text{penalty on within-line spread}},
  \label{eq:mil-decomposition}
\end{equation}
since the cross term sums to zero. The first term is the error of the line's mean prediction, the second
the spread of the predictions within the line. The per-cell model minimises both, so it is pushed to
give all cells of a line the same prediction. A multiple-instance (MIL) model with mean pooling
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-mil}) minimises only the first term.
% (\code{notebooks/4b\_mil\_training.ipynb}, \S2)

Of the two standard MIL formulations (Section~\ref{sec:bg-mil}), the instance-level one was chosen over
embedding-level attention pooling, although the latter has been argued to classify bags better \cite{ilse2018}: the
question is whether individual cells receive different predictions, and only
the instance-level form produces that quantity. A bag was one complete cell line --- every sequenced cell,
not a sample --- since the label is defined per line and only a complete bag removes the
variance term (Appendix~\ref{app:milcrit}). The bag prediction was the
mean of its cells' predictions, matching the label's definition, and the evaluation includes a
positive control as mean pooling can be minimised by collapsing a bag onto its mean. The
alternatives that were considered and not used are given in Appendix~\ref{app:milcrit}.

The evaluation asked whether the MIL model gives the cells of one line different predictions, and whether
those differences are reproducible across seeds and not explained by sequencing quality or the cell cycle.
It had five stages, each judged against a null or a comparison rather than a fixed threshold. The
\emph{input ceiling} checked that the cells of a line differ in the input at all, reported as the ratio
of between-line to within-line variance. The \emph{positive control} mixed the cells of two lines with
slightly different response, from the lowest quartile of a compound's pairwise response differences,
into one bag, and tested whether the per-cell predictions separate them, by the AUROC against shuffled
line labels \cite{mannwhitney1947,hanley1982}. The \emph{spread} stage required the predictions within a
line to vary more than under the per-cell model. The \emph{reproducibility} stage required different
seeds to order a line's cells alike. The \emph{confound} stage vetoed a pass if sequencing depth,
detected genes, mitochondrial fraction and the two cell-cycle scores \cite{scp542} explained as much of
that variation as the agreement between seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:confounds}). As a description rather than a
test, the predictions were also correlated within each line with the heterogeneity programs identified in
the source study, to see which known variation within a line they follow (Appendix~\ref{app:milcrit}).
