\section{Introduction}

Tumour heterogeneity is a major driver of drug resistance and treatment failure. Rare subpopulations of
tumour cells may underlie resistance \cite{scp542}, the few cells that survive treatment can ultimately
cause relapse \cite{scdeal}, and in one clinical trial the predicted response of the most resistant clone
separated responders from non-responders better than an average over clones \cite{perception}. Drug
response, however, is measured on bulk populations of cells, and no large-scale screen measures it per
cell (Sections~\ref{sec:bg-screens} and~\ref{sec:bg-singlecell}). Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)
resolves the subpopulations that bulk measurements average over \cite{luecken2019}, so a model that
predicts response from single cells could report a distribution of sensitivities within a sample rather
than one average \cite{perception,scdeal}.

This report trains such a model on the labels that exist. The response labels of the Cancer Therapeutics
Response Portal v2 (CTRPv2) \cite{ctrpv2,ctrp_rees}, like those of PRISM \cite{prism} and the Genomics of
Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) \cite{gdsc_yang2013,gdsc}, are measured on bulk cell populations. They are mapped onto the individual
cells of the corresponding cell lines in the SCP542 pan-cancer atlas \cite{scp542}, so that one value per
(cell line, compound) is broadcast onto several hundred heterogeneous cells, and the setup is therefore
weakly supervised. Cell lines do not fully recapitulate tumours, but they provide the volume of
continuous labels needed to train a representation, and they are heterogeneous in expression: in this
atlas, distinct genetic subclones were detectable in 26\,\% of the lines, and 39\,\% of the discrete
expression clusters were associated with them, while the continuous expression programs largely were not
\cite{scp542}. The cost is a mismatch of resolution. One bulk value per line adds label noise to every
cell, and because all cells of a line share it, they are pseudo-replicates, so the effective sample size
is the number of cell lines rather than the number of cells \cite{lazic2010,dreval}.

The cells are represented in two ways, by principal component analysis (PCA) fitted to the atlas
\cite{jolliffe2016,luecken2019} and by the frozen embedding of scGPT, a transformer pretrained on about
33 million human cells \cite{scgpt} (Section~\ref{sec:bg-foundation}). The hypothesis behind the
comparison was that scGPT provides a denoised biological prior which aligns cellular states across
tissues, so that a regressor is encouraged to learn transcriptomic drivers of sensitivity rather than to
memorise the tissue or line of origin. Single-cell counts, by contrast, are sparse, with many zeros due to
sampling \cite{luecken2019}, and the premise was that regressing them directly onto bulk labels would be
unstable. A latent-space comparison illustrates the difference between the two representations
(Figure~\ref{fig:umap}). PCA separates the atlas into compact islands, whereas scGPT places the same cells
on one continuous manifold. The islands are individual cell lines, the granularity at which the response
label is defined, so the figure motivates the hypothesis without testing it. The three research
questions this design addresses are set out in Section~\ref{sec:bg-objective}.

The scope of this report is a single database, a single continuous response score and a supervised
regressor, trained on a panel of \NPanel{} compounds and controlled against the unselected catalogue of
\NDrugsModelled{} \cite{ctrpv2,dreval}. Every result is read against a ridge regression on cell-line
means \cite{hoerl1970}, against the naive baselines of the DrEval benchmark, and with general cell-line
sensitivity controlled out, because DrEval shows that published drug-response models often score well
through compound and cell-line mean effects alone \cite{dreval}. Cross-database integration, toxicity and
clinical fine-tuning are left to later work.

Section~\ref{sec:background} introduces the biological and computational background and states the
objective. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the data, the representations, the model and its
evaluation, Section~\ref{sec:results} reports and discusses the results for the three questions, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with the next steps.
