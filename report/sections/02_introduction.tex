\section{Introduction}

Tumour heterogeneity is a major driver of drug resistance and treatment failure: relapse is typically
caused not by the average cell of a tumour but by rare, pre-existing subpopulations that survive
treatment and repopulate it \cite{scp542,perception}. Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) averages over
exactly these subpopulations, whereas single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq) resolves them
\cite{scp542,luecken2019}. Predicting response per cell would let a model report a distribution of
sensitivities within a sample and, in principle, identify resistant subclones before treatment selects
for them \cite{perception,scdeal}. The long-term goal of this project is a pan-cancer single-cell
foundation model that predicts pharmacological response, both efficacy and toxicity, and can be
fine-tuned to specific cancer types or to clinical data with binary outcomes \cite{scgpt,perception}.
The immediate obstacle is that no large-scale single-cell drug screen exists \cite{scdeal}.

The setup is therefore weakly supervised. The response labels of the Cancer Therapeutics Response
Portal v2 (CTRPv2) \cite{ctrpv2,ctrp_rees}, like those of PRISM \cite{prism} and GDSC \cite{gdsc}, are
measured on bulk cell populations, and they are mapped onto the individual cells of the corresponding
cell lines in the SCP542 pan-cancer atlas \cite{scp542}, so that one value per (cell line, compound) is
broadcast onto several hundred heterogeneous cells. Cell lines do not fully recapitulate tumours, but
they provide the volume of continuous labels needed to train a representation, and they are clonal in
origin yet heterogeneous in expression, so any within-line structure is transcriptional state rather
than lineage \cite{scp542}. The cost is a mismatch of resolution: one bulk value per line adds label
noise to every cell, and because all cells of a line share it they are pseudo-replicates, so the
effective sample size is the number of cell lines rather than the number of cells \cite{lazic2010,dreval}.

Three research questions follow. The first is whether drug response can be predicted from single cells
with bulk labels at all. The second is whether embeddings from scGPT, a transformer pretrained on
roughly 33 million human cells \cite{scgpt}, are a viable representation for the task, measured against
the standard dimensionality reduction, principal component analysis (PCA) \cite{jolliffe2016,luecken2019},
at matched width. The third is whether a model trained on single cells learns cellular heterogeneity
\emph{implicitly}, as a by-product of mapping many transcriptional states onto one outcome. The third is
the clinically relevant question, and it is the reason the analysis is carried out on cells rather than
on cell-line averages: a model that reproduces only the population mean cannot see a surviving
subpopulation, however accurate that mean is \cite{scp542,perception}.

The hypothesis behind the second question is that scGPT provides a denoised biological prior which
aligns cellular states across tissues, so that a regressor is encouraged to learn transcriptomic drivers
of sensitivity rather than to memorise the tissue or line of origin; regressing sparse single-cell
counts directly onto bulk labels is, by contrast, unstable \cite{scgpt,luecken2019}. A latent-space
comparison illustrates the difference between the two representations (Figure~\ref{fig:umap}): PCA
separates the atlas into compact islands, whereas scGPT places the same cells on one continuous
manifold. The islands are individual cell lines --- the granularity at which the response label is
defined --- so the figure motivates the hypothesis without testing it. The approach differs from
PERCEPTION \cite{perception}, which pretrains on bulk data to predict PRISM response, and from scDEAL
\cite{scdeal}, which transfers from bulk to single cells, in evaluating a frozen single-cell foundation
embedding directly against PCA, in a design that extends to multi-task learning across databases.

The scope of this report is a single database, a single continuous response score and a supervised
regressor, trained on a panel of \NPanel{} compounds and controlled against the unselected catalogue of
\NDrugsModelled{} \cite{ctrpv2,dreval}. Every result is read against a ridge regression on cell-line
means \cite{hoerl1970}, against the naive baselines of the DrEval benchmark, and with general cell-line
sensitivity controlled out, because DrEval shows that published drug-response models often score well
through compound and cell-line mean effects alone \cite{dreval}. Cross-database integration, toxicity and
clinical fine-tuning are left to later work.
