\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results are interpreted per research question, followed by a conclusion; the limitations that
bound these readings are collected in Section~\ref{sec:limitations} \cite{dreval}.

\paragraph{Prediction from single cells with bulk labels.} Drug response is predictable from these data,
but modestly, unevenly, and not demonstrably from single cells. The score is out of fold over held-out
lines, stable across seeds, and clears DrEval's naive predictors on a protocol this work did not design
\cite{dreval}. Against that, a ridge regression on cell-line means reaches the per-cell model, and a
per-compound random forest matches it externally, so two structurally different line-level models reach the
same score and the single-cell resolution and the network are not what does the work
\cite{hoerl1970,breiman2001}. On the unselected catalogue the signal nearly vanishes, which marks the
panel's score as a property of eleven well-covered compounds rather than of the screen
\cite{ctrpv2,dreval}.

\paragraph{What limits the score.} The spread across compounds is explained on the label side: the share
of flat curves predicts the per-compound score without recourse to biology, which agrees with the known
inconsistency of pharmacogenomic labels \cite{haibekains2013,bayer2023curvecurator}. With one label in nine
carrying no dose--response and roughly \NLinesCV{} independent lines, the binding constraint is the supply
and quality of labels, not model capacity \cite{lazic2010}. The relation is a trend over eleven points,
and crizotinib shows it is not a law. Cisplatin's position at the bottom was anticipated by the
literature on its determinant (Section~\ref{sec:panel}) \cite{friboulet_ercc1}.

\paragraph{How much of the score is compound-specific.} About a fifth of what either representation ranks is
general cell-line fragility, in nearly the same proportion for both \cite{dreval}. Under LPO, where a
baseline can use a line's average response, that baseline alone reaches the score of this model; the
LCO protocol used here hides the channel by construction, because a held-out line has no estimated
effect \cite{dreval}. The reported score is therefore real but partly not compound-specific.

\paragraph{scGPT against PCA.} scGPT is a viable representation but falls behind PCA here, and the
leading account is a mismatch of granularity rather than a deficit of the embedding. The label is
defined per cell line, so what a model must read is variation between lines; PCA carries about a fifth
more of that structure before any model is trained, and Figure~\ref{fig:umap} shows the same difference.
A pretrained single-cell embedding is built to map cells onto a shared manifold and to suppress
cell-line and batch identity \cite{scgpt}, so on this label it removes exactly the structure the label
varies over. Four rival accounts are eliminated: the margin grows rather than shrinks with more labels,
which contradicts label starvation; capacity lowers both arms; rescaling the input does not change
training, as expected of AdamW, which divides each step by a running second moment of the gradient
\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}; and PCA stays ahead once fragility is removed. Eliminating rivals does not
establish the granularity account, whose positive evidence is one variance ratio and one figure. The
margins are conservative with respect to input dropout, since the shared rate costs the PCA arm and not
the scGPT arm \cite{srivastava2014}. This is a statement about evaluation design --- a representation
built to remove cell-line identity scored on a label that is cell-line identity --- not about scGPT in
general \cite{scgpt,dreval}.

\paragraph{Heterogeneity learned by a single-cell model.} The question remains open. The MIL model
separates a line's cells reproducibly and not as a sequencing artefact, but it does not separate two
lines known to differ: its positive control is barely above chance, so the instrument cannot carry a
negative result and makes the positive one weak \cite{ilse2018,mannwhitney1947}. The PCA arm clears the
confound veto at \QBarPca\,\% of the bar, so its within-line signal is not confound-dominated rather than
confound-free, and whether any of the variation is drug-response heterogeneity cannot be decided without
a per-cell response measurement \cite{scp542}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} A general-purpose foundation embedding loses to a dataset-fitted linear reduction
here because the label sits at the granularity the foundation model is built to suppress, and with
\NLinesCV{} labels a ridge regression on cell-line means matches everything built in this work: the
ceiling is the data, and the analysis identifies which part of the data sets it
\cite{scgpt,hoerl1970,dreval}.
