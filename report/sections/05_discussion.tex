\section{Discussion}

\revision{\textbf{Rewritten 12.08.2026.} This section previously argued from measurements that have since
been withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:results}). What follows is restricted to what does not depend on them:
the conditions the design must satisfy, each of which was arrived at by a failure rather than by
foresight. Where a claim would need a number, it is stated as an open question rather than answered.}

What the work has established so far is a set of prerequisites for the comparison it set out to make, and
each was found by getting it wrong first.

\textbf{The objective must not be captured by a minority of compounds.} In a shared multi-task loss with
one masked squared-error term per drug, a compound's contribution scales with its response variance, so a
few high-variance compounds can dominate the shared representation regardless of whether that variance
carries any learnable signal. An early configuration collapsed to near-zero correlation for this reason
and was misread as a statement about the representations. Whether the correction belongs in the target,
in the loss, or in the composition of the panel is not settled --- per-drug standardization of the target
was adopted and then retired, because it silently rescales a noise-dominated compound up to full weight,
which is a defect of its own.

\textbf{The control must already absorb the cell-line effect.} A per-drug-mean null is too weak a bar:
some cell lines are broadly sensitive, and a model can score a respectable per-drug rank correlation by
learning that alone. The appropriate control is a ridge regression on cell-line mean embeddings, which
uses no single cells and no network, and the informative question is whether the single-cell apparatus
clears it. This is the cell-line-effect bias the DrEval study \cite{dreval} was designed to expose. It
also has a sharp consequence for the metric: under a leave-cell-line-out protocol, DrEval's normalization
removes only the drug effect, because a held-out line's effect is by construction unknown to the
baseline. A high normalized score is therefore not evidence of drug-specific signal in this setting, and
separating the two requires a diagnostic that reads held-out labels rather than a metric.

\textbf{The compounds must be selected without reference to our own labels.} Two successive panels were
selected on response statistics --- a gate requiring a compound both to kill and to spare cell lines, and
a literature panel whose candidates had been pre-ranked on the same quantity --- and both were withdrawn.
The first excluded every cytostatic agent by construction; the second retained the label dependency it
was built to remove. The panel is now defined by regulatory approval and published determinants
(Section~\ref{sec:panel}), which is reproducible by someone who never sees our response values.

\textbf{The effective sample size is the number of cell lines.} The label is defined per cell line and
drug, so all cells of a line carry the same broadcast value and act as pseudo-replicates. The task is
defined on roughly \NIndep{} independent examples, not on \NCells{} cells, and treating cells as
independent understates every uncertainty by roughly the square root of the number of cells per line.
This bounds what any architecture on this data can be expected to demonstrate, and it is the reason the
next phase acts on the labels and the data rather than on the model.

Read together, these are not preliminary steps toward the representation comparison. They are the finding
of this stage, and the comparison is deferred until it can be made under conditions that would let it
mean something.
