\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work asked three questions about predicting drug response from single cells with bulk labels.

Drug response can be predicted in this setting, but modestly and unevenly. The highest-scoring
configuration reached \SpBest{}, the spread across compounds followed the share of flat dose--response
curves, and about a fifth of the ranking was general cell-line sensitivity. A ridge regression on
cell-line means scored about as well as the default per-cell configuration, \RidgePca{} against
\SwPcaMaeZero{}, so nothing in this work shows the per-cell pipeline earning its complexity
(Section~\ref{sec:rd-q1}). This agrees with benchmarks in which simpler models kept pace with more
complex ones: elastic nets outperformed neural networks for most compounds on GDSC \cite{eckhart2024},
and under leave-cell-line-out evaluation no model surpassed a tuned random forest \cite{dreval}.
PERCEPTION, on the same atlas, likewise obtained predictive models for only 44 of 133 drugs
\cite{perception}.

scGPT embeddings are a viable representation, but a weaker one than PCA fitted to the atlas, in all six
configurations. Before any training the scGPT inputs already carried less between-line structure, and
since the label is defined per cell line, this is the leading account of the gap (Section~\ref{sec:rd-q2}).
A zero-shot evaluation of scGPT and Geneformer came to the same ordering for cell types, which their
frozen embeddings separated worse than a selection of highly variable genes \cite{kedzierska2025}. The
comparison has a clear limit, however. scGPT was used frozen, although its reported results were
obtained after fine-tuning \cite{scgpt}. Fine-tuning was not attempted because about \NLinesCV{}
labelled lines are few for adapting a pretrained encoder, which then risks overfitting to them.
Single-cell profiles with matching response labels exist here for \NLines{} cell lines, against
\NCtrpLines{} lines labelled in CTRPv2 alone \cite{dreval_data}. What this work tests is therefore the
frozen embedding under the current data situation, not scGPT in the use its authors intended.

Whether a model trained on single cells learns cellular heterogeneity implicitly remains open. A
multiple-instance model gave a line's cells reproducibly different predictions that are not dominated by
sequencing artefacts, but its positive control did not reliably detect a known, slight difference in
response (Section~\ref{sec:rd-q3}). The prior methods closest to this work face the same limit. scDEAL
and PERCEPTION also evaluated per-cell predictions through labels defined for groups of cells
\cite{scdeal,perception}. As long as drug response is measured per well rather than per cell, whether a
model reads response from single cells can be checked only indirectly.

These results also bear on how drug response prediction is evaluated more generally. DrEval found that
reported performance is often overly optimistic, because the mean effects of compounds and cell lines
can carry a score \cite{dreval}. The present results confirm that such mean effects make up a large part
of what is scored. Pooled over all pairs, the highest-scoring configuration reached \SpPooled{}, but a
predictor that knows only each compound's average response already reached \SpDrugMeanPooled{}. Within
compounds, about a fifth of the score was general cell-line sensitivity, and under LPO a line's average
response alone reached \LpoLineMean{}, above the best configuration here. On all \NDrugsModelled{}
compounds rather than the eleven of the panel, the score fell to \SpAllDrugs{}, and that model's squared
error was \HdRiseLinPca\,\% higher than that of a constant per compound. A score reported without these
comparisons would therefore have overstated what the model learned. What these results cannot confirm
is DrEval's further finding that about half of the tested models do not outperform a predictor of mean
effects, because only one model family was tested here, and under LCO the cell-line baselines score
zero by construction.

Whether models or data limit drug response prediction cannot be settled by this setup, but every
comparison made here points to the data. The effective sample size is the number of cell lines, about
\NLinesCV{}, not the number of cells \cite{lazic2010}, and a ridge regression on cell-line means matched
the per-cell network. The share of flat curves predicted the per-compound score at $\rho = \FlatRho{}$
without recourse to biology, and screens disagree on the response of the same line to the same compound
\cite{haibekains2013,safikhani2017}. No model can recover a response that its label does not contain.
The models tested here were small, however, and scGPT was used frozen, so a limit on the model side is
not excluded. More complex models may pay off once the data are richer. In the NCI-DREAM challenge,
which combined several bulk profiling data types per cell line, the best methods modelled non-linear
relationships \cite{costello2014}.

From the perspective of this work, three things are missing to make drug response prediction from
single cells viable. The first is more cell lines profiled at single-cell resolution, since labels
already exist for several times as many lines as were profiled. The second is labels of known quality:
curve fits that report whether a dose--response was established at all \cite{bayer2023curvecurator},
and responses that agree across screens. The third is a response measured per cell rather than per
well. The screens label the viability of a whole population, so they cannot say which of its cells
survive. The single-cell chemical screens that exist record the transcriptome after treatment rather
than survival \cite{srivatsan2020,tahoe100m}, and PERCEPTION's evaluation against the response of
patients rested on 28 patients of one trial \cite{perception}. Until such data exist, the binding
constraint is the supply and quality of labels rather than the model.

Two hypotheses follow from these results and remain untested. The general cell-line sensitivity
within the score may partly reflect proliferation rate, since the AUC depends on the number of
divisions the cells undergo during the assay \cite{hafner}. The per-cell model may also be limited by
the absence of bulk pretraining, which in PERCEPTION's linear models on the same atlas gave the weaker
result \cite{perception}.

The next steps address the model's objective, the label bottleneck and the third research question.

\paragraph{MIL as the primary model, with a ranking loss} A model trained on bags of a line's cells
leaves each cell free to differ (Equation~\ref{eq:mil-decomposition}), and since the metric ranks lines
within a compound, a ranking loss would align the objective with what is scored
\cite{ilse2018,burges2005}.

\paragraph{Bulk pretraining and domain adaptation} DrEval's release of CTRPv2 alone covers \NCtrpLines{} cell
lines against the \NLines{} here, so labels exist and single-cell expression is what is missing
\cite{dreval_data,scdeal}. Pretraining on bulk expression and aligning bulk and single-cell spaces by domain
adaptation addresses that gap \cite{scdeal,ganin2015}. The most direct form is to apply PERCEPTION's
bulk-pretrained response models to these cells and compare their predictions with those from the two
representations here \cite{perception}.

\paragraph{Combining label sources} CTRPv2 is one of several screens that label the same kind of cell
lines, and GDSC and PRISM measure response with different assays and readouts
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-screens}) \cite{gdsc_yang2013,corsello2020}. A model could be trained on several of them at
once, with one output per screen and compound, so that each screen's labels inform the shared
representation. This would broaden the data the model is trained on and make it less dependent on the
particularities of a single screen's labels. Because screens disagree on the same line and compound
\cite{haibekains2013,safikhani2017}, labels could also be weighted by their agreement across screens or by the quality
of their curve fit, which CurveCurator reports per curve \cite{bayer2023curvecurator}.

\paragraph{Fine-tuning scGPT, given more data} Fine-tuning tests whether the ordering against PCA
reverses once the encoder may keep line identity, but on about \NLinesCV{} labels a fine-tuned encoder
may memorise them, so it follows the previous step.

\paragraph{Explicit explainability of heterogeneity} The question could be reframed from implicit
learning to explicit explanation: identifying the subpopulations within cell lines that drive resistance
and relating them to the recurrent heterogeneity programs annotated for this atlas \cite{scp542}.

\paragraph{Missing controls} Every comparison here is between two reductions of one matrix. A
line-level model on the \NHvg{} selected genes alone would show whether either reduction helps at all. Division rate, the first hypothesis above, would be extracted as fragility is, from
measured doubling times rather than from the dataset's cell-cycle scores, which are a snapshot rather
than a rate. The input-dropout rate could be matched in effect rather
than in value, by choosing per arm the rate that removes the same expected variance
\cite{srivastava2014}.
